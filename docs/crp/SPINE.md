## The spine

> Older adults increasingly need help with the ordinary tasks of daily living, while
> the professional workforce that could provide that help, the family capacity that
> once absorbed it, and the means to pay for it are all shrinking. When needed support
> is not established, the consequences compound: preventable emergency care and
> hospitalization, caregiver breakdown, and premature institutionalization, each
> deepening the very needs that went unmet (the vicious cycle of unmet need,
> Figure 1). Families in this position do not fail for lack of information. They fail
> between identifying appropriate care and establishing it: a pathway that runs from
> recognizing a need, through confirming what care fits and what aid, insurance, or
> family resources can pay for it, to reaching a provider with actual capacity and
> starting the arrangement. Many sources of help support parts of that pathway
> (clinicians and discharge planners, information and benefits tools, referral
> services, family and friends), but each one's role ends before the pathway does,
> and none is accountable for whether care is actually established. Nor can the
> pathway be completed from the family side alone: care is established only when a
> provider can say yes, and providers increasingly cannot, with 63.3 percent of
> home-care providers declining cases in 2023 for lack of staff. Across SBIR Phases I
> to IIB, Olera built and validated the care-identification half of this pathway: an
> eldercare AI navigation system over an expert-curated national database that
> screens a household's needs and matches it to aid, services, and providers, with
> acceptance validated among family caregivers; the remaining Phase IIB year
> completes that layer through production integration of the agent system, an n=200
> evaluation, and database expansion beyond 100,000 records. The CRP funds the
> care-establishment half, which remains unproven and which no private investor will
> yet fund: execution and follow-up capabilities that carry each family to
> established aid and care; concentration of families, providers, and caregiver
> workforce in selected local markets until care connections complete reliably; and a
> test of the commercial hypothesis that two provider products, Managed Ads and
> Staff Recruitment, addressing the client-acquisition and staffing problems
> providers already pay to solve, can generate the recurring revenue that sustains
> the platform while families pay nothing and connections remain free. The award
> succeeds when an independently verified model shows each market's provider revenue
> covering the cost of serving its families: the evidence that makes Olera
> investable, scalable, and permanently free for the families it serves.

## The strategic test

Every major claim, aim, experiment, milestone, figure, and commercialization activity
in the application is tested against this paragraph:

**Does it move a family from identifying care to establishing it, or prove that
someone will pay for the machine that does?**

If yes, preserve or strengthen it. If no, understand why it is there before carrying
it forward.

## Vocabulary register

- **Application prose:** care identification / care establishment; "from recognized
  need to established care." Terms verb cleanly (identify, establish) and extend the
  existing house endpoint language ("aid or care established").
- **Internal shorthand only, never in application prose:** "knowing vs. having,"
  "the knowing half / the having half."
- **Timeline framing, always:** current state → remaining Phase IIB work (production
  agent integration, n=200 evaluation, 100,000+ records) → CRP work → mature
  commercial model. The CRP begins where Phase IIB reasonably ends and re-funds none
  of it.
- **Deliberately unspecified at spine level:** market count ("selected local
  markets"; the number follows from the Approach redesign), the specific workforce
  intervention ("caregiver workforce"; the student program appears later), market-size
  dollar figures (placeholders live in `strategic-context-2026-08-20.md` §7).

## Open language questions (refinable; not architecture)

1. Whether "the care-identification half / the care-establishment half" appears
   symmetrically twice, or the second occurrence relaxes to "what remains:
   establishing care."
2. Whether "permanently free for the families it serves" survives into external
   prose or stays internal. It is a vow, and vows in NIH documents get quoted back.
