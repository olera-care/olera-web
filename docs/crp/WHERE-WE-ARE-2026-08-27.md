Working expectation: Significance 2-3 confident; Innovation currently 4-5, reachable to 2-3 with TJ's pass; Approach not there yet, most within our control; Environment the one we influence least.

**Innovation went from weakest to possibly strongest** (TJ). The system learns from every completed case and gets better at the next, with real technical substance. The workforce innovation has aged: what was novel two years ago is now done by several organizations. TJ: *"We want it to be obviously awesome. And right now, it's not obviously awesome."*

**The objection that can sink us.** TJ: *"If someone understands entrepreneurship and notices the gulf between us and economic viability, and just calls bullshit. You don't have revenue. Y'all been at this for how many years? You have $5 million."* TJ also named the review-room mechanism: a skeptic raising an objection others had not considered can pull scores two to three points during discussion, after initial scores are in.

**David's substantive challenge.** *"I'm actually quite skeptical about this physician only payable model."* Outpatient senior-care providers run thin margins and a fixed subscription is the wrong instrument. Two consequences adopted:
- **Price on value delivered, not a flat fee.** His examples price on utilization (employer pays when an employee uses the service, 6-7% utilization; tele-dentistry charges when an exam is taken). TJ's parallels: ZocDoc charges on a successful transaction; Airbnb and Upwork take a rate and own the payment rails; Yelp monetizes placement.
- **Keep more than one revenue path visible.** Not to build them all, but because a single path reads as fragile.

**What David said is already working, and should be left alone:** the problem articulation (comprehensive and in depth) and the Phase I-IIB continuity. He also liked the figures.

## 2. Decisions

- **A fourth gate: execute it.** Qiping identified the gap. The work between finding care and receiving it is administrative (applications, documentation, intake, confirmation), which is exactly what the agent layer does and we were not naming it. New sequence: **find it, afford it, execute it, staff it.**
- **Staffing is the beachhead for two reasons:** the only thing sellable now, and the bottleneck that makes navigation fail.
- **The payer opportunity is a secondary endpoint, never a primary aim.** TJ: *"getting the data to prove to someone that we will be valuable, and making that research activities ... it just kills prose. It's like you're running on a track, and then you just run into quicksand."*
- **Aim 3 gets simpler.** Four-price randomized market assignment dropped; Qiping raised contamination and brand risk (providers talk to each other). One price informed by Aim 2 willingness to pay. Price adjustment becomes a contingency (promotions, trial offers), not a design. One wave.
- **No HSR in Aim 3.** Keeps us clear of the clinical-trial definition.
- **Criteria-driven market selection.** From David's Times Square Health story: first hire was a CTO who scanned 3,300 counties at zip level on CMS and pop-health data for MA penetration and payer consolidation. Rochester: 85% MA eligibility vs 55% national, four payers hold 85%. Chicago has fifty payers. David: *"at least be deliberate on paper. Why we pick sort of market."*
- **No campus stratification.** We have no hypothesis that students outperform other workers, so stratifying tests nothing.
- **Pre-health students are not themselves the innovation.** CareYaya and others already connect students to families. The innovation is the system bringing new capacity into a local market through licensed providers, with the verified record as the mechanism. Add CareYaya to the competitive table.
- **Aim 2 measures value created; willingness to pay is load-bearing.** ~40 families and ~40 providers at about four weeks. Families: did it get you aid and care (primary), did it prevent poor outcomes (secondary). Providers: did you convert families to clients, and did you employ enough staff to stop turning care away. Sharpened: *"Did you have to deny care to anybody we sent you?"* Providers also asked what they would pay; that plus cost to serve sets the Aim 3 price.
- **National baselines, not a control arm.** Qiping proposed National Inpatient Sample and comparable surveys for readmission, admission and length-of-stay baselines. Logan explicit: no two-arm trial.
- **Most Phase IIB references come out of the Approach.** Qiping: *"you don't want them to think, oh, you still have the money ... and now you're asking for extra money."*
- **Qiping moves to co-PI.** Effort unchanged; budget and forms implications to work through.
- **Aim 1 metrics cut to three, at most five.** "Field-level agreement" is the wrong term pre-field.
- **No more full-document handoff passes.** TJ: *"I don't think we should do the pass thing. That's kind of done."* Converge by talking; edit in the live document.
