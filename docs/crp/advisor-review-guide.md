# NIH Commercialization Readiness Pilot (CRP): Advisor Review Guide

**Full application solicitation here: https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/ed6db6b2-d74e-4838-9223-ce72f4895688** (PAR-27-098, SBIR/STTR Commercialization Readiness Pilot Program, Parent SB1). *Summary below for reviewer orientation.*

**What the CRP is.** The CRP provides up to three additional years of NIH-supported development toward commercialization for SBIR/STTR technologies that completed Phase II or IIB but still need late-stage development to reach the market (award ceiling $4,191,495 in total support). Full commercialization must ultimately continue after the CRP on non-SBIR resources; the solicitation instructs: "All applicants are expected to describe a realistic plan (extending beyond the CRP), which outlines how and when full commercialization can be accomplished. The full commercialization of the product/technology should be carried out with non-SBIR funds." The proposed activities should therefore leave the company able to attract third-party investment, sustain itself through revenue, or both.

**What reviewers are asked to evaluate, document by document.**

- **Specific Aims (1 page).** The bridge between the Research Plan and the Commercialization Plan: each aim should remove an important remaining barrier to commercialization, carry measurable outcomes, and together leave the product able to continue commercializing without SBIR funding.
- **Research Plan (12 pages).** How the proposed work leads to a marketable product or service, with quantitative milestones; the significance of the problem and commercial opportunity, and the innovation relative to existing approaches; a rigorous approach with benchmarks, potential problems, and alternative strategies; and a progress report showing how prior phases reduced commercialization risk.
- **Commercialization Plan (12 pages).** A realistic path extending beyond the CRP: the remaining commercialization gap and why NIH funding is needed now; product, customers, market, and competition; IP position; the finance plan and revenue model; and realistic, market-based five-year milestones.

**The Fundraising Plan and the investor letter, in NIH's own words.**

> "The NIH considers the raising of independent third-party investor funds to be an important means to facilitate and accelerate the capital-intensive steps that are required to commercialize new products/technologies emerging from NIH-funded SBIR/STTR Phase II projects. Applicants are expected to provide a Fundraising Plan, which should be a detailed and specific plan for securing substantial, independent third-party investor funds if they are necessary to bring the product to commercialization. If they are not needed, the company should detail how they are able to bring the technology to market without the use of third-party investor funds."

> "In addition to standard letters of support documenting collaborations and access to expertise or unique research resources, applicants should include letters of support documenting commitments from third-party investors to support the Fundraising Plan section if applicable."

The application guide lists the acceptable forms of this evidence: "Letter of commitment of funding. Letter of intent or evidence of negotiations to provide funding, should the Phase II project be successful and the market need still exist. Letter of support for the project and/or some in-kind commitment (e.g., to test or evaluate the innovation). Specific steps you are going to take to secure Phase III funding." What matters is that the letters credibly support the Fundraising Plan.

**How scoring works.** Reviewers score on NIH's 1-9 scale (1 = exceptional, 9 = poor). Five core criteria receive individual scores; CRP-specific considerations inform the single Overall Impact score, which is not an average: "the likelihood for the project to exert a sustained, powerful influence on the relevant market offering." The specific criteria reviewers are given are summarized below, in NIH's wording.

**Significance** (scored): If the aims are achieved, how will the field improve? Does the project have commercial potential to lead to a marketable product? How compelling is the value proposition, and to what extent is there substantial market-pull? How well are market segments, customers, the competitive environment, customer needs, and acceptance hurdles understood?

**Investigators** (scored): Is the team well suited to the project? Do prior experience and qualifications lend confidence the team will succeed in commercializing the product; how successful have they been in commercializing technologies in the past?

**Innovation** (scored): Is the product novel to the field? Will it shift current practice paradigms? Is it substantially innovative compared to all existing approaches as well as those in development?

**Approach** (scored): Are strategy, methodology, and analyses well-reasoned and appropriate to the aims? Are potential problems, alternative strategies, and benchmarks for success presented? How appropriate are the milestones? Is there a detailed, feasible Project Management Plan?

**Environment** (scored): Are commitments of partners, subcontractors, and consultants documented? How well can the company sustain itself and grow as a business? How successful is its track record commercializing prior SBIR/STTR projects?

**Commercialization Plan** (informs Overall Impact): Has the applicant identified realistic, market-based milestones achievable over the next five years? How reasonable are the plans for generating a revenue stream, and how realistic are the projections?

**Fundraising Plan** (informs Overall Impact): Will third-party investors play an active role in facilitating commercialization, and to what extent? If third-party funds are not needed, how well does the application support the company's ability to bring the technology to market? If needed, how well does it support the ability to secure substantial independent third-party investor funds?

**IP Position** (informs Overall Impact): How strong is the applicant's intellectual property portfolio and position pertinent to the proposed project?

**Regulatory Path** (informs Overall Impact): How sound is the proposed Regulatory Plan to meet Federal regulatory requirements?
