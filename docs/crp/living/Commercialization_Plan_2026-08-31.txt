The product and its impact. The gap between older Americans 
who need help and the care available to them is growing. An 
aging population is increasing demand while families continue to 
face a fragmented system for finding providers, securing financial 
assistance, and establishing care. When care is not established, 
the consequences can compound into a vicious cycle of unmet 
need, hospitalization, failed care establishment, and premature 
institutionalization

1).

24%).1 Olera developed CareNavigator through NIA Phase I–IIB 
(Impact Scores 20 and 25) to intervene earlier in this cycle by 
helping families identify appropriate care, find aid to pay for it, and 
connect with providers who can deliver it. Its central outcome is

The remaining opportunity is to make the full care-establishment 
pathway computable, executable, and measurable from recognized need through confirmed aid or care (Figure 
2). Prior work substantially developed the upstream functions needed to assess household needs and means, 
identify likely benefits and aid, and match appropriate services and providers. The CRP adds three integrated 
capabilities: (1) a longitudinal Care Establishment Model that represents needs, means, funding, service, 
execution, capacity, and outcome as structured case state; (2) bounded AI agent workflows that execute and 
learn from the administrative work required to move that state toward established care; and (3) data-directed 
Caregiver Staffing that adds new workforce capacity when insufficient labor prevents care establishment.

Why government funding is the right instrument at this stage. Crossing this gap requires later-stage R&D 
and evidence generation before CareNavigator's largest commercial pathway can be demonstrated. Private 
investors must underwrite the risk of completing and deploying the system before its institutional value has 
been established, while institutional buyers need real-world evidence before they can confidently value and 
purchase the product. Non-dilutive CRP funding can break this cycle by financing the work needed to retire 
these risks. 
The alternative is not simply to raise prices or sell the same product differently. Charging families would create 
the greatest barrier for households already struggling to afford eldercare. Charging providers for referrals would 
introduce steering incentives and limit participation by some federally reimbursed providers. Caregiver Staffing 
can generate nearer-term provider revenue without those tradeoffs, but staffing alone addresses only the 
workforce barrier; it does not help families navigate, fund, and execute the rest of the care-establishment 
pathway. 
Our investor advisors agree that longitudinal outcomes demonstrating CareNavigator's value to institutional 
buyers, together with a repeatable provider-revenue model, would materially improve Olera's investability. 
How CRP funding advances Olera to full commercialization. Three sequential aims retire the remaining 
risks. Aim 1 engineers and technically verifies the integrated care-establishment infrastructure and establishes 
its safe automation envelope. Aim 2 tests that system across eight free validation markets, measuring verified 
care establishment, agent execution, stakeholder value, workforce throughput, acquisition, and operating cost. 
Aim 3 then enters eight new paid markets to test conversion, retention, repeatable market activation, and live 
unit economics while producing the institutional evidence package and post-CRP payer proof-of-concept 
specification. 
2. Value of the CRP Project, Expected Outcomes, and Impact 
The product to be commercialized. CareNavigator is Olera's family-facing eldercare navigation platform. It 
combines a national, expert-curated resource database with AI-supported execution workflows and longitudinal 
outcomes tracking to help families move from recognized need to established care. Families use 
CareNavigator at no cost. The platform assesses needs, identifies appropriate care and financial aid, helps 
execute the administrative and follow-up work required to obtain them, confirms whether care was established, 
and records where the pathway succeeds or fails (Figure 4). 
Caregiver Staffing is a complementary provider-facing product and capacity mechanism. When an otherwise 
appropriate care plan cannot be delivered because a provider lacks workers, Olera recruits new caregivers into 
the workforce and connects them with licensed providers, which retain responsibility for interviewing, hiring, 
training, credentialing, supervision, and care delivery.

care-establishment pathway and creates evidence about what happened at every step. AI-supported execution 
moves the product from recommending what a family should do toward completing and tracking the work 
required to establish care, and every executed case produces a structured longitudinal record of the pathway. 
At scale, this longitudinal record could become a distinctive commercial and scientific asset: a county-level 
empirical map of where eldercare pathways succeed, where they fail, and what resolves those failures. The

(Figure 4, lower register).

3. Company 
Origins and objectives. Olera, Inc. grew from a multidisciplinary effort at Texas A&M University to solve a 
problem families repeatedly described: eldercare was difficult to navigate, difficult to afford, and difficult to 
convert from information into actual support. PI Tokunbo (TJ) Falohun began working with Logan DuBose, MD, 
MBA, now Olera's Chief Research Officer and co-investigator, through the Texas A&M chapter of Sling Health 
in 2019. Their initial work focused on dementia caregiving; continued discovery revealed a broader need

Olera's corporate objective is to make establishing eldercare navigable and broadly accessible for American 
families while building the commercial infrastructure that can sustain that access. The commercialization 
strategy has evolved as evidence accumulated; the mission has not. Core CareNavigator access remains 
free to families, recommendations remain neutral, and commercialization is concentrated where Olera creates 
incremental value for providers and institutional buyers.

4. Market, Customer, and Competition 
Market segments and potential customers. Olera commercializes through two buyer classes created by the 
same care-establishment pathway. The near-term beachhead is care-delivery providers that lose revenue 
when caregiver vacancies prevent them from accepting or staffing new cases. The emerging institutional 
market is healthcare organizations that bear financial risk when unmet needs contribute to avoidable utilization, 
failed care transitions, or earlier institutional care. 
Caregiver Staffing addresses an unusually persistent provider problem. The United States employed 
approximately 4.68 million home health and personal care aides in 2025, with roughly 760,500 openings 
projected each year from 2025 to 2035. Home-care benchmarking separately reported a median 
professional-caregiver turnover of 75% in 2024.5,6 The problem directly constrains growth: 63.3% of surveyed 
home-care providers reported turning down cases due to staffing shortages in 2023.7 Caregiver Staffing 
therefore addresses provider workforce demand. Non-medical home care is Olera's initial provider beachhead. 
The institutional market is larger but evidence-gated. Prospective customers include Medicare Advantage 
plans, accountable care organizations (ACOs), health systems, Medicaid managed care and managed 
long-term services and supports organizations, and other entities exposed to the downstream costs of unmet 
need. In 2026, 35.2 million people are enrolled in Medicare Advantage, and 14.3 million Medicare beneficiaries 
receive care coordinated through accountable-care arrangements.8,9 CMS's active GUIDE Model further 
validates the purchasing logic as Medicare is already testing and paying for dementia care navigation, 
community-resource connection, and caregiver support, with the explicit aim of delaying nursing-home 
placement.10 
Market opportunity and path to meaningful scale. Caregiver Staffing enters an existing, recurring 
recruitment market in which providers already spend to fill vacancies. The CRP determines Olera's repeatable 
pricing, conversion, retention, and unit economics. The CRP helps determine whether CareNavigator leads to 
more established eldercare and models if that outcome could create sufficient economic value for institutional 
contracting by reducing utilization of costly healthcare resources. Olera’s geographic unit of commercialization 
is a county-centered local market which is defined as a service area anchored to one focal U.S. county or 
county equivalent, within which family demand, provider participation, workforce capacity, care-establishment 
outcomes, acquisition costs, and commercial performance are attributed and measured. With more than 3,100 
such geographic units nationally, Olera can expand by replicating a validated local-market model without 
requiring dominant national market share.

Caregiver Staffing's initial workforce wedge is deliberately narrow. Olera initially targets health-profession 
applicants and students for whom paid caregiving can also provide meaningful patient-care experience. The 
opportunity is nationally distributed and continuously replenished: in the most recent cycles, U.S. MD programs 
reported 54,699 applicants, and NursingCAS reported 75,078 applicants across 282 participating nursing 
schools.11,12 These two pipelines are not the full addressable workforce, as we plan to design a system that 
can eventually incorporate career changers, gig workers, and other well-positioned new labor pools into the 
direct care workforce. Traditional recruiting channels primarily compete for workers already searching for 
caregiver jobs. Recent recruitment benchmarking found Indeed generated 68% of applications to participating 
home-care agencies in Q1 2026, illustrating how concentrated conventional caregiver acquisition remains.13 
Olera instead builds relationships with universities and applicant communities to introduce caregiving as a paid 
entry pathway into healthcare, while the licensed provider remains the employer responsible for interviewing, 
hiring, training, credentialing, supervision, and employment standards. 
Current and emerging competition. For Caregiver Staffing, Olera competes across categories. Provider 
staffing alternatives include large job boards like Indeed, staffing agencies, caregiver-specific recruiting 
platforms, and emerging student-caregiver models such as CareYaya. For CareNavigator, alternatives include 
government and nonprofit resource directories, patient navigators, social workers and care managers, 
eldercare referral platforms like A Place for Mom or Caring.com, and increasingly general-purpose AI and 
search where families ask eldercare-related questions. The full customer segment and competitive 
environment is described in the Research Strategy Significance section.

5. Intellectual Property Protection 
Protection strategy. Olera’s defensibility does not depend on a single patent claim. The principal commercial 
moat is the accumulated combination of non-public execution logic, domain-specific data architecture, 
geographically conditioned operational knowledge, longitudinal care-establishment records, workforce 
infrastructure, and distribution channels required to reproduce the system. These assets receive different forms 
of protection according to how value is created. Source code and execution logic are restricted as trade 
secrets and protected by copyright. Proprietary database structure, derived variables, quality-control methods, 
field observations, and longitudinal datasets are access-controlled and governed through confidentiality and 
data-use obligations. Product names and branding receive trademark protection where appropriate, and 
CRP-generated inventions with separable patentable value will undergo counsel review before public 
disclosure. Olera will maintain an invention and disclosure review process throughout the award so that new 
workflow, data-modeling, execution, or workforce methods are evaluated for trade-secret versus patent 
protection before publication or other public disclosure.  
Compounding barriers to replication. A new entrant would need to recreate Olera’s national provider and 
benefits infrastructure, family distribution, Care Establishment Model, program- and provider-specific execution 
workflows, local field knowledge, workforce channels, provider relationships, and the longitudinal record of 
which pathways actually succeeded or failed. These assets compound through use. Each executed case adds 
timestamped operational observations, exposes missing or stale local knowledge, tests workflow behavior, 
contributes care-establishment outcomes, and improves the geographic model used by subsequent cases. 
Because this information largely does not exist on the public web and cannot be reconstructed retrospectively, 
the time required to accumulate comparable executed-case history becomes part of the moat. 
6. Finance Plan 
Capital required. Olera is requesting approximately $4 million in CRP funding over three years to finance the 
later-stage R&D, real-world validation, and commercialization work required to cross the Valley of Death 
described in Section 1. The award is designed to move Olera from pre-scale commercialization to an 
investable commercial inflection point, not to assume that revenue from the limited CRP markets immediately 
replaces the full federal operating budget. 
From CRP capital to commercial sustainability. Olera’s financing strategy deliberately separates evidence 
generation from commercial scale. During the CRP, federal capital finances the engineering, real-world 
validation, and market experiments that private investors are not yet positioned to underwrite. Caregiver 
Staffing begins paid testing in eight new markets in Year 3, producing live conversion and unit-economics data. 
Task 3.2 simultaneously produces the evidence package required to secure a post-CRP institutional 
proof-of-concept. Independent third-party capital then finances expansion of the validated Staffing model and 
the payer validation step needed before institutional CareNavigator revenue is assumed. Commercial revenue 
progressively takes on a larger share of growth only after each revenue engine demonstrates its economics. 
Fundraising Plan. Olera has deliberately financed its development primarily through non-dilutive SBIR support 
and has not yet completed a material institutional equity financing. The post-CRP raise would therefore 
represent an important transition in the company’s capital structure, and the CRP is designed to generate the 
evidence required to make that transition on substantially stronger terms than are available today. Investor 
cultivation will begin by the end of Year 2, after technical verification and early real-world evidence are 
available. A formal financing process will begin during Year 3 as paid-market conversion, Staffing throughput, 
retention, unit economics, and the institutional evidence package mature. The objective is to enter post-CRP 
commercialization with independent financing secured or actively closing rather than encounter a new Valley of 
Death at award completion. 
Investor engagement. Commercialization advisor David Qu will advise Olera quarterly throughout the CRP, 
helping management define investment-readiness milestones, pressure-test the financing strategy and 
materials, and prepare for institutional fundraising. As milestones mature, he mayl support introductions and 
continued engagement with relevant senior-care and healthcare investors in his network, including Ziegler, 
Equitage Ventures, 7Wire Ventures, and Alumni Ventures, among others. His Letter of Support describes this 
role and commitment to helping Olera prepare for and pursue independent third-party financing as CRP 
milestones are achieved.​
​
Financing readiness is milestone-based rather than calendar-based. The financing case will be built 
around evidence that is unavailable today: (1) technical verification of bounded agent execution and the Care 
Establishment Model; (2) verified care establishment across up to sixteen validation markets; (3) observed 
agent-execution share and human-escalation burden; (4) measured Staffing acquisition, placement, retention, 
and hires per market-month; (5) paid provider conversion, retention, CAC, cost to serve, and contribution 
margin across eight new commercial markets; (6) evidence that the market-entry playbook transfers beyond 
the founders; and (7) a contracting-ready institutional evidence package and payer proof-of-concept 
specification. 
Post-CRP financing requirement. Olera expects independent third-party capital to finance the transition from 
CRP validation to commercial scale. With approximately $1.4 million in expected operating requirements in 
Year 4 and roughly $90,000 in modeled commercial revenue, and approximately $1.85 million in Year 5 
operating requirements against roughly $400,000 in modeled revenue, Olera expects a substantial post-CRP 
financing requirement. The current target of approximately $3–5 million will therefore be refined from the 
CRP’s measured Staffing economics, demonstrated market-entry costs, institutional proof-of-concept 
requirements, and desired expansion pace rather than from optimistic revenue assumptions.

whether demonstrated provider value converts into real purchasing. The Aim 2 markets may continue 
operating and may later generate revenue, but they are excluded from the primary Aim 3 paid-conversion 
analysis because their prior free exposure could influence purchasing behavior.

8. Revenue Stream 
Two revenue engines, sequenced by evidence. CareNavigator remains free to families, and basic 
family-provider connections remain free to providers. The near-term engine is Caregiver Staffing where 
providers pay when Olera helps them successfully hire workers. The emerging engine is institutional 
CareNavigator contracting to help reduce utilization of costly healthcare services for payer member 
populations. 
Caregiver Staffing revenue follows successful hires. Successful hires x Realized revenue per successful 
hire x Active county markets is the equation used to estimate revenue potential. In Year 2, Olera deploys 
Staffing free across eight validation markets to establish applicant acquisition, provider hiring, placement, 
retention, market throughput, and geographic reproducibility before price is introduced. In Year 3, paid testing 
begins in eight new markets that did not receive free Aim 2 exposure. The working base-case price for this 
model is $250 per successful hire, with $150 and $350 as sensitivity bounds. This is an Aim 3 pricing 
hypothesis, not an asserted market price. It is economically plausible relative to the burden providers already 
bear in hiring care workers (Table 7).

The pre-CRP financial model therefore uses an illustrative and conservative base case of two successful hires 
per active market per month. At the $250 per hire charged to the provider as our Aim 3 pricing hypothesis, the 
base case equals $6,000 annualized Staffing revenue per active market and $48,000 across eight paid 
markets. Aim 2 will replace the throughput assumption with observed hires per market-month before Aim 3 
pricing and paid conversion are interpreted. 
The longer-range scenarios illustrate geographic replication rather than forecast a mature national throughput 
rate. At the two-hire-per-market-month base case, 100 active markets would produce approximately 2,400 
successful hires and $0.6 million in annual Staffing revenue; 250 markets, approximately 6,000 hires and $1.5 
million; and 500 markets, approximately 12,000 hires and $3.0 million. We estimate a total addressable market 
of 1233 mid-sized counties, representing ~66% of the 3000+ counties in the US.  
Institutional CareNavigator: contracts follow outcomes evidence. The institutional buyer class is 
illustrative of the pre-CRP base case. CRP years 1 to 3 test whether recognized needs reach established care, 
why pathways fail when they do not, and what happens longitudinally afterward. This matters economically 
because unmet home- and community-based service needs have been associated with substantially greater 
acute-care utilization (Section 1).8 There is already precedent for organizations responsible for health 
outcomes to pay for care-management and coordination infrastructure: CMS's GUIDE Model uses 
per-patient-per-month dementia care-management payments for coordination and caregiver support, and in 
2026 the Medicare Shared Savings Program includes 511 ACOs serving 12.6 million Traditional Medicare 
beneficiaries.9,10 These sources establish the buyer class and purchasing logic but they do not establish 
Olera's future price. Accordingly, the base case assumes no institutional revenue during the CRP or the first 
post-CRP proof-of-concept year in Year 4. Institutional revenue enters the base case only after proof of 
concept in Year 5, beginning with one approximately $250,000 annual relationship.

Team, workstream ownership, and governance. Falohun retains final go/no-go authority and owns product, 
engineering, agent-execution QA, and provider commercialization. DuBose owns research integration,

guidance. An existing full-time family/provider operations team member will serve as Market Operations Lead
