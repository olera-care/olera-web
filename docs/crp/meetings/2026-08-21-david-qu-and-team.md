## 1. The strategic shift (the headline)

**LOCKED in principle, drafting implications SOLVING.** The mock review's principal
reservation (the revenue products could be sold without the marketplace) was read to
TJ verbatim and he agreed with it. The resolution chosen is the opposite horn from
the coupling-clause fix the mock review proposed:

- The free navigation marketplace is the **traction, inbound, and upsell engine** for
  the two premium provider products. Say it plainly rather than defending liquidity.
- **Hurdles to adoption re-anchors on providers buying and keeping Managed Ads and
  Staff Recruitment**, not on marketplace liquidity. TJ: "liquidity as the main KPI
  would make sense if we're selling leads. We're not."
- Avoid the word "liquidity" (finance jargon); prefer marketplace health or provider
  and family concentration where the concept is still needed.
- **The CRP hypothesis, crystallized by both founders:** can a platform that never
  charges for leads sustain itself on premium provider offerings (the Yelp/Facebook
  model), with pre-committed alternatives if the answer is no. A future business-model
  experiment (transaction rails with a take rate) was named for the CP only, not the RS.
- **Free connections upgraded from mission constraint to innovation claim** with three
  legs: (1) no gating of a vulnerable population from help; (2) the Anti-Kickback
  Statute is WHY incumbents serve only private-pay assisted living and why no
  comprehensive navigation exists ("private capital has only invested in what is
  legal to get leads off of" - twist this knife); (3) anti-steering / in-network bias.
- Staffing framed as the high-margin play that can subsidize the free network
  (Logan estimate in-meeting: $20-30K revenue per placed student per year; verify
  before any document uses it).

**Ripple effects (SOLVING, nobody owns yet):**
- The Approach overall-design rationale must be re-derived: the sharp question is
  "are you doing the activities that lead to selling the products, or building a
  healthy marketplace because mission?" Aim 1's family-acquisition spend needs a
  justification that blends impact (NIA cares about families) + provider-acquisition
  economics (providers arrive when families ask; 15x activity on lead days) + product
  value (qualified referrals drive retention). Never phrase as "the marketplace is a
  funnel for ad sales" in grant prose.
- Aim 3's cross-side analysis (lagged family demand vs provider retention) becomes
  supporting evidence for the upsell thesis rather than the central hurdle test.
- **SPINE.md tension, flagged not edited:** the locked spine says concentration
  "until care connections complete reliably" and the award-success framing leans on
  the marketplace. The meeting demoted liquidity as the commercial hurdle. The spine's
  family-side (identification/establishment) is untouched; the provider/commercial
  paragraph likely needs a ratified amendment. Reopen with Logan only.
