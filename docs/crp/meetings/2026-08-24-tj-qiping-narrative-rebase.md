## Substantive convergences reached (not just presentation)

1. **Staffing-forward product story; trim Managed Ads emphasis.** The
   marketplace itself is the free client-acquisition value; the flagship paid
   product is Staff Recruitment, positioned as what lets providers actually
   say yes (the capacity problem). TJ's takeaway sentence: "Narratively it's
   cleaner to talk about staff... we probably trim emphasis on Managed Ads."
   Other tools get mentioned in passing as premium tools under exploration.
2. **"Managed Ads" naming is politically risky.** Qiping: it "sounds biased...
   we are not maintaining our neutral standpoint." TJ: "we don't want to
   trigger box checkers." Working replacement: **Boost** (Logan's CP term,
   visibility boost), described well once, then referenced.
3. **Pricing methodology for Aim 3** (sketched and liked): free pilots of the
   premium tools during Aims 1-2; exit interviews establish qualitative
   willingness to pay (entrepreneur-style pricing interviews); Aim 3 runs real
   billing at the interview-derived anchor plus/minus a step, measuring
   conversion, retention, churn. Real billing stays in Aim 3; interviews feed
   it rather than replace it.
4. **Aim 3 simplification.** Qiping trims methods complexity. Watch item: the
   simplification must not remove the real-billing market test itself; the
   meeting's own pricing design depends on it.
5. **Two products stated at one level of detail:** a staffing product
   (pre-health students, defended against alternatives) and a client-growth
   product (non-referral mechanisms; described once). This reshapes the old
   two-vs-three flag: the resolution now trending is staffing primary, Boost
   secondary, conversion folded into the free loop or mentioned in passing.
