# Aims 2–3 Study Design Sketch under Architecture C (2026-08-18)

Conceptual-design artifact — not grant prose. Grounded in the current RS (Qiping's
design, snapshot 2026-08-17) and the codebase evidence map. Illustrative numbers are
marked; floors and thresholds are set with the biostatistician and baseline data, never
invented here. Companion: `aim2-aim3-architecture.md` (Architecture C rationale).

## 0. The three kinds of questions, separated

| Kind | Question | Answered by | With what |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scientific | Does each product create its intended, measurable provider value? | **Aim 2** (Task 2.2 field test) | per-product value-endpoint estimation with CIs, under eligibility + evaluability rules |
| Product | Is each product mature and usable enough to sell? | **Aim 2** (Task 2.1 verification + implementation outcomes) | end-to-end verification; SUS, AIM/IAM/FIM, workload, support minutes |
| Commercial | Will providers buy it at economics that sustain the company? | **Aim 3** (entirely) | real billing: conversion, retention, revenue, cost to serve, CAC, LTV, payback, margin |

Aim 2 also *measures* one commercial input — provider acquisition cost (Task 2.3) —
because it is generated by the acquisition machinery Aim 2 runs; but no willingness-to-
pay or revenue question is asked before Aim 3.

## 1. Participant flow (approximate; enrollment rolls in waves)

```
PRELIMINARY EVIDENCE
  Staffing paid pilot (fees paid by franchisees of 4 national brands; volumes from
  Logan's consolidated pilot record) · Managed Ads operating with named campaigns ·
  ~725 onboarded providers incl. ~15 Growth Suite signups · ~2,400 family leads/mo
        │
        ▼
AIM 2  (Years 1–2, twelve markets)  — "Does the Suite create measurable value?"
  Task 2.1  VERIFY  each product's full path end-to-end (no human subjects)
  Task 2.2A FORMATIVE usability: ~30 provider staff + ~20 students (human subjects)
  Task 2.2B FIELD TEST: ~80 provider accounts (home care + senior living strata;
            ~20% experienced-user cap) + ~100 students, ≥6 months per account
            · all accounts receive the full Suite free
            · product exposure = prospective eligibility × natural activation
            · platform instruments everything; research instruments sample people
  Task 2.3  SCALE: channelized provider/student acquisition, wave gates, cost models
        │
        ▼
READINESS DECISION  (mid-Year 2, aligned with the CP's paid-conversion gate)
  each product → PRICE-READY | VALUE-PENDING | NOT ADVANCING
  (pre-registered decision rules: evaluability + value floor + demand signal +
   operational readiness; defined in §4)
        │
        ▼
AIM 3  (Years 2–3, original + ~6 paid-first markets) — "Will providers pay enough?"
  offering: Growth Suite subscription = bundle + tiers built from the PRICE-READY set;
            VALUE-PENDING products included free, clearly labeled, still measured
  cohorts:  CONVERSION (~60–70 Aim 2 accounts offered paid plans)
            PAID-ENTRY (~120–140 new providers, paid terms from first contact)
            ≈200 accounts total, followed 12 months under real billing
  Round 1 pricing (Van Westendorp-seeded arms across matched markets)
  Round 2 (pre-registered): repackaging + late-maturing products join
        │
        ▼
END-OF-AWARD EVIDENCE PACKAGE
  measured prices, conversion, retention curves, revenue per account, cost to serve,
  CAC, LTV:CAC, payback, market-level sustainability — CPA-rebuilt; revenue quality
  BY PRODUCT; both market-entry playbooks; feeds the CP break-even story
```

## 2. Aim 2 protocol sketch

**Population.** Provider *organizations* in the private-pay segments the products
serve: non-medical home care agencies and senior living / assisted living communities —
two pre-specified strata, reported separately, never powered as subgroup claims. Other
segments (adult day, home health) admissible, not targeted. The human-subjects
*participants* are the staff who use the products (owners/administrators,
recruiters/schedulers, marketing/intake staff) and the student caregivers; the
*economic unit* is the account. (This person/account separation already exists in
Qiping's Aim 3 text — extend it to Aim 2.)

**Eligibility.** Account level: licensed provider organization operating in one of the
twelve markets; claimed profile; a designated staff user; consent to research use of
platform data. Product level (prospective, recorded — this is what makes denominators
meaningful): Staffing → active campus pipeline in the market and a stated hiring need;
Visibility → profile-completeness threshold (this gate already exists in the product);
Conversion → active inquiry flow (≥N leads/month from any source; N set from baseline
data). Ineligible: unlicensed operators; Olera-affiliated parties.

**Prior exposure.** Both experienced and new providers enroll, as a pre-specified
stratum, with experienced users (existing Growth Suite signups, pilot providers) capped
at ~20% of accounts. Rationale: excluding early adopters discards the best-instrumented
accounts and the preliminary-work continuity; including them uncapped inflates
estimates. The structural guard against early-adopter bias is Aim 3's paid-entry
cohort, which replicates the offer de novo in fresh markets.

**Sample size (~80 accounts).** Sized for precision, not hypothesis tests: with ~80
activated accounts, the suite-level value-delivery proportion carries a 95% CI
half-width of ≈11 points; a product with the minimum ~20 evaluable accounts carries
≈20–22 points — adequate for decision rules whose floors sit far from the boundary,
and stated at exactly that strength. Operationally: ~6–7 study accounts per market.
Final numbers with the biostatistician. (~100 students with 20% attrition allowance —
current RS spec, preserved.)

**Recruitment.** Through Task 2.3's actual machinery: claim-your-profile outreach to
the in-market indexed base, national brand and association relationships, business-line
calls, paid digital, conferences, direct mail; research e-consent at activation.
Students via campus advisors, health-professions organizations, digital outreach.

**Intervention and duration.** Free access to the full Suite for every activated
account; product use is natural, never assigned. Each account is followed ≥6 months —
long enough to observe first value (expected within 8–12 weeks per pilot experience),
repeat cycles, and 60-day sustainment; staffing placements are followed to 90 days;
students for 3 months (semester-aligned). Waves are scheduled so accounts enrolled by
end of Year 1 contribute ≥6 months of exposure before the readiness decision.

**Exposure reality (providers use what they need).** Expected and welcome: the usage
mix is itself a study output — it becomes the attach-rate priors for Aim 3 packaging.
Evaluability rules (below) decide which products have accumulated enough exposure to be
evaluated at all.

**Instrumentation.** Platform-automatic (all verified to exist): activation and module
events, leads delivered/opened and lead outcomes, review requests and posted reviews,
campaign delivery and attributed inquiries, applicant/interview/hire events, verified
shift logs, support contacts, timestamps throughout. Research instruments: SUS
(primary usability), AIM/IAM/FIM (acceptability/appropriateness/feasibility), NASA-TLX
re-scoped to staffing and intake tasks, baseline role/organization survey,
semi-structured interviews (providers and students; non-activators, one-time users,
and retained users all sampled — preserved from current design). Cost records: channel
spend and staff minutes.

**Pre-registered value endpoints** (starting point per Logan; floors set with
baseline + biostatistician):

| Product | Value endpoint (account-level) | Why a reviewer believes it is value |
|---|---|---|
| Staffing | ≥1 hire with a first verified paid shift within 90 days of first qualified applicant; co-metrics: qualified applicants, time-to-hire, 30/90-day retention | a completed hire is what staffing agencies charge thousands for; the pilot showed providers paying $275/month for exactly this |
| Visibility | ≥[floor] attributable qualified inquiries within 60 days of activation; supporting: posted verified reviews, profile completeness attained | a qualified inquiry is what incumbent aggregators sell for $3,000–6,000 per placement; attribution is UTM-based and already implemented |
| Conversion | ≥1 admission/service start from a tracked lead carried through the follow-up workflow within 60 days; co-metrics: scheduled assessments, response latency | an admission is the provider's revenue event itself; the product's only job is producing it from leads of any source |

**Suite-level primary endpoint:** proportion of activated accounts reaching ≥1 product
value endpoint within 90 days (Wilson 95% CI), with 60-day repeat use as the
co-registered demand signal. This is the number the whole aim stands on.

**Secondary outcomes.** Per product: endpoint rates among evaluable accounts,
time-to-first-value, repeat use, workflow abandonment, support minutes, workload.
Suite: SUS, AIM/IAM/FIM, attach rates by segment and stratum, acquisition cost per
activated provider by channel (Task 2.3). Students: application→interview→hire funnel,
verified hours, 30/90-day retention, experience interviews.

**Analysis.** Estimation, stated as estimation: proportions with CIs; GEE (logit,
provider-organization clustering, small-sample corrections — preserved from current
design) to describe correlates (segment, size, prior-exposure stratum, market family-
demand density); Kaplan-Meier time-to-first-value; CFIR-informed qualitative matrix
with joint displays (preserved). No between-product efficacy comparisons — the current
RS already disclaims this correctly; keep the disclaimer.

**Heterogeneity discipline.** Two strata, stratified reporting, segment as a
descriptive covariate — and nothing else. No sub-segment claims.

**Human subjects in Aim 2.** Provider staff: yes (consent, surveys, interviews).
Students: yes (consent, monthly follow-up, the existing employment-separation
protections — preserved verbatim in spirit). Families: no — family research lives in
Aim 1 Task 1.2; family platform data used in Aim 2 only as de-identified operational
metrics. Task 2.1 verification: not human-subjects research (internal QA).

## 3. What we keep / modify / remove from the current design

**Preserved (Qiping's machinery):** two-stage validation (formative → field);
saturation stopping rule; role-based purposive sampling; SUS + AIM/IAM/FIM instrument
set; GEE with organization clustering and small-sample corrections; market as
reporting stratum; mixed-model sensitivity analysis; deliberate inclusion of
non-activators and disengagers; student protections and research/employment
separation; Task 2.1 verification with outside-record reconciliation; Task 2.3 wave
discipline, channel decision rules, and cost models; ~100-student cohort with
attrition allowance.

**Modified:** intervention = one Suite with three products (not four modules); referral
network moves to the free base layer, measured as cross-side context; sampling
arithmetic redone around the role × product grid (the "five users per module per role
group" implies 45–60, not 30 — restate honestly); NASA-TLX re-scoped (staffing +
intake; "referral" scope removed); eligibility and evaluability rules added; a
suite-level primary endpoint added; endpoint definitions per the table above;
prior-exposure stratum added; duration rationale stated; decision point becomes
readiness states.

**Removed:** independent per-module statistical graduation gates; "4 of 4 modules"
counts; Table 1 artwork's four-module language (rebuild); "three of the four solve the
problem without family volume" logic (superseded).

## 4. The readiness decision (end of Aim 2 → entry of Aim 3)

Logan's illustrative triad is right, made precise. Each product is assigned, by
pre-registered rule at the readiness review (mid-Year 2), exactly one state:

- **PRICE-READY** — all four conditions met: (1) *evaluability*: ≥~20 eligible
  accounts with ≥~8 weeks' exposure [final numbers with biostatistician]; (2) *value
  floor*: endpoint rate among evaluable accounts at or above the pre-registered floor;
  (3) *demand signal*: repeat use at 60 days at or above its floor; (4) *operational
  readiness*: Task 2.1 verification passed and support load sustainable.
  → **Consequence:** the product enters Aim 3 Round 1 as a priced tier and bundle
  component.
- **VALUE-PENDING** — evaluability not yet met, or met with floors unmet but
  qualitative evidence identifies addressable barriers. → **Consequence:** stays free
  in Build-Measure-Learn inside the pilot; re-assessed at the pre-registered Round 2;
  included in the offering as a clearly-labeled free beta (contributing attach-rate
  data, not billed).
- **NOT ADVANCING** — evaluability met and the evidence is affirmatively negative
  (value and demand floors missed, qualitative work confirms no addressable barrier).
  → **Consequence:** not priced during the CRP; its working components are absorbed as
  retention features of the paid tiers; the CP's per-market revenue model recomputes.

The floors themselves (e.g., whether the current RS's ≥70% value-endpoint bar is a
readiness floor or an aspirational target) are set with Qiping and the biostatistician
against baseline data — a decision, not an inheritance.

## 5. Aim 3 protocol sketch

**Who participates.** Two cohorts, both preserved from the current design, now with
explicit sourcing: the **conversion cohort** — Aim 2's activated accounts (~60–70
after attrition) offered paid plans in the original twelve markets; the **paid-entry
cohort** — new providers (~120–140) enrolled under paid terms from first contact in
the ~6 paid-first markets plus continued enrollment in the original markets. ≈200
accounts total. Person-level research participants (surveys ~150–250, interviews ~30)
are consented separately from the account-level economic units — preserved.

**What providers are offered.** The Growth Suite subscription: a bundle plus
single-product tiers, composed of the price-ready set. Value-pending products ride
along free, labeled as such. Products are therefore tested *both* individually (tiers,
per-product Van Westendorp lines) *and* bundled (bundle arms) — the packaging
experiments already in Task 3.1 do this work.

**How prices are chosen.** Van Westendorp survey (~120 decision-makers with purchase
authority, drawn from both cohort frames) defines the acceptable range; the arms enter
at those points; conditions are assigned across matched markets (not accounts, because
neighboring providers compare notes — preserved); baseline conversion is recorded
first; the biostatistician sizes arms. The primary contrast — paid conversion within
60 days of offer — is the commercial act itself, which is what keeps the design from
becoming academic: the experiment *is* the selling.

**Follow-up.** 12 months of real billing per account. Retention at 3/6/9/12 months by
cohort; discrete-time survival with competing events (voluntary cancellation, payment
failure, downgrade); restricted mean survival time at 12 months → expected account
lifetime → LTV with confidence intervals — all preserved.

**Outcomes.** Primary: paid conversion within 60 days of offer. Secondary: retention/
churn; revenue per account; cost to serve; CAC (all acquisition spend, including
non-converters, divided by accounts won — the honest denominator); monthly net account
margin; LTV:CAC; payback; market-level sustainability threshold; cross-side liquidity
(value and retention against lagged qualified family demand); AIM/IAM/FIM;
explanatory interviews across non-converters / churned / retained-low / retained-high.

**Sample rationale.** ≈200 accounts sized by expected churn events (60–100) for the
survival models and by 95% CI half-widths ≤7 points on key proportions — Qiping's
existing specification, preserved and now explicitly tied to the two-cohort structure.

**Commercial success (pre-registered).** A market is viable when payback ≤12 months
and LTV:CAC ≥3:1 with margin at or above the derived sustainability threshold; award-
level success adds the cross-side bar — steady-state provider revenue covers the
Aim 1 cost of serving families — with the failure case pre-committed for honest
report. (All current-RS bars, preserved; the *count* of viable markets required is an
open decision flagged for the Aim 3 pass.)

**Early-adopter bias protection.** Structural: every economic estimate is reported by
cohort, and the paid-entry cohort — providers who never saw a free product — is the
replication test. A large conversion-vs-paid-entry gap is itself a finding that
reprices the playbook.

**Human subjects vs commercialization.** Dual-track, stated plainly: pricing, billing,
and selling are commercial operations; the surveys, interviews, and any analysis
linking platform/billing records to person-level responses run under IRB with study
IDs — the current RS already draws this line; keep it explicit at the top of the aim.

**What exists after Aim 3 that did not exist after Aim 2.** Prices providers actually
pay; conversion and retention curves; realized revenue per account; measured cost to
serve and CAC; LTV, payback, margin; market-level sustainability determinations; and
an independently rebuilt (CPA) unit-economics model. Aim 2 ends knowing *value*;
Aim 3 ends knowing *money*. Those measured parameters replace the CP §9 model's
hypotheses (mature-market revenue, serve cost, conversion), which is how the award-end
break-even story stops being a projection and becomes an audited claim.

## 6. Pressure tests

1. **Staffing strong, Conversion weak** → readiness states diverge; Round 1 prices
   what's ready; Conversion stays free → Round 2 or absorption. Nothing stalls. ✔
2. **All three offered, only one used** → usage mix is data (attach-rate priors);
   evaluability rules convert "no exposure" into *value-pending*, never into a fake
   negative. Suite primary (≥1 endpoint) is unaffected. ✔
3. **Home care and senior living use it differently** → two pre-specified strata,
   stratified reporting, segment covariate; Aim 3 packaging can tier by segment
   without new studies. Fragmentation capped by design (two strata, no more). ✔
4. **Early adopters outperform new providers** → capped experienced stratum +
   stratified reporting in Aim 2; cohort-separated economics in Aim 3. A gap revises
   the acquisition playbook rather than contaminating the estimates. ✔
5. **Value strong, willingness-to-pay weak** → the pre-registered Task 3.1B ladder
   (unbundle → re-tier → add value where interviews locate the gap → Round 2). If it
   persists: the product stays free as a liquidity asset and the CP model recomputes —
   a reportable, honest outcome the design anticipates. ✔
6. **Willingness-to-pay strong, cost to deliver too high** → the margin term catches
   it (monthly net account margin, cost to serve measured under TDABC); response is
   automation (Task 2.3's rising-automated-share metric exists for exactly this),
   price/packaging adjustment, or non-scaling. Managed Ads' human-in-the-loop
   execution is the live case this protects against. ✔
7. **Suite-powered, product claims underpowered** → by construction: product-level
   results are estimates with stated precision feeding decision rules — the design
   never claims independent statistical proof of three products. Language discipline
   enforced at drafting. ✔

**Residual weaknesses found while trying to break C (disclosed):** (a) if *two*
products land value-pending, Round 1 prices a thin offering — acceptable (single-
product launch + free betas is a normal commercial posture) but the CP's per-market
revenue would need its Round-2 contingency stated; (b) the readiness review's timing
must respect wave maturity — solved by requiring decision-time evaluability rather
than calendar-uniform exposure, with late waves feeding Round 2; (c) Conversion, as
the product built *during* the CRP, is structurally the most likely value-pending
candidate — the proposal should say so upfront as sequencing logic (build → verify →
validate → price later), which turns a weakness into a plan.
