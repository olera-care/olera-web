The Approach As Written

If we changed absolutely nothing, this is the study we are currently proposing: concentrate family and provider recruitment in twelve markets chosen to differ in family demand. In Years 1–2, Aim 1 verifies and validates the family-facing CareNavigator and builds active family acquisition with measured costs, while Aim 2 verifies and validates a four-module Provider Growth Suite with ~80 provider accounts and ~100 students, building provider and student acquisition in the same markets. Each offering graduates individually into Aim 3 when it meets its readiness endpoint. Aim 3 (Years 2–3) then prices the graduated offerings through pre-registered experiments on two cohorts — converted free pilots and new providers acquired in new markets under paid terms — and measures conversion, retention, and unit economics, independently rebuilt by a CPA, against pre-registered bars.

Where marketplace-building currently lives

Four places, no single home: the Approach preamble ("we concentrate family and provider recruitment in twelve markets that differ in family demand"); Task 1.3A (family channels + the market-selection criteria: families arriving · providers · aid rules · campus); Task 2.3A (provider and student channels + "Aim 1's funnels seed local family demand"); and Problems-and-alternatives ("insufficient family liquidity in a market: reallocate or increase Aim 1 family acquisition"). The concept is present and consistent in spirit — but it is described piecemeal, never named as one system, and the liquidity each product needs is nowhere specified.

Reconciliation: the converged improvements against this baseline

What already fits

More than expected. Both aims already run build/verify → validate → adopt (the task structure *is* the pipeline). Aim 3 already prices what graduates, on two cohorts, with a pre-registered second round. Acquisition is already operational discipline, not research. Per-offering graduation already exists. The 12-county concentration with demand variation is already the design's spine. Our converged model is a simplification of this text, not a replacement.

What conflicts

Five things: four modules vs three products; the 80-account prospective field study as the *only* value evidence (vs the operational stream carrying it); no willingness-to-pay anywhere in Aim 2 — which means the current design decides what to price with no commercial-plausibility screen at all; the conversion cohort built from research pilots (vs grandfathering them and offering upgrades to the non-research free base); and the referral network priced as a module while the CP declares leads free.

What is unnecessarily complicated

Per-module statistical graduation gates; the module × role sampling grid; some of 2.2B's trial machinery (GEE as primary rather than as a tool inside an observational plan); NASA-TLX scoped to a module that is leaving the Suite; and marketplace prose repeated in four places.

What is genuinely missing

Willingness-to-pay collection in Aim 2; the plausibility screen at the gate; the operational-stream analysis plan (value definitions and denominators); a named marketplace infrastructure block with per-product liquidity prerequisites; the paid-entry cohort's market count and geography in the RS; and the single reconciled number chain (counties → accounts → payers → revenue → sustainability).

Where Aim 1 fits — the unifying insight

Aim 1 is not a sibling study bolted on; it is the same pipeline applied to the family side of the marketplace, with one difference: its commercial test is not payment but cost. Families never pay; Aim 1 proves the demand engine works and measures what a family costs to acquire and serve — the number Aim 3's cross-side bar divides provider revenue by. One sentence tells the whole Approach:

Aim 1proves we can create family demand, affordably, and establish care
Marketplacethat demand + provider and student acquisition = liquidity in 12 counties
Aim 2proves the products turn that liquidity into provider value, free
Gatevalue + plausible economics → a real price
Aim 3proves providers pay more than the whole system costs

Where ongoing acquisition should live

Keep channel detail where it is (families in 1.3A, providers and students in 2.3A — each aim owns its side's machinery), and add one short marketplace block in the Approach preamble that names the shared infrastructure, states county selection and build order, and defines each product's liquidity prerequisite. The tasks then cross-reference it. One home, two owners, no repetition.

What would make the three aims one continuous story

  1. State the shared pipeline once in the preamble and map each aim onto it (the strip above).
  2. Name the marketplace as continuous infrastructure with per-product prerequisites.
  3. Migrate Aim 2 to the three products; free referral layer stated as such.
  4. Move willingness-to-pay into Aim 2's cohort; add the plausibility screen at the gate.
  5. Make Aim 3's entry explicit: what is priced, who is offered what, where — including a derived (not inherited) market count for paid entry.
  6. Reconcile the number chain once, at the end, across RS, CP, and Aims.

Six changes. Everything else in the current Approach survives — most of it verbatim.

Honest finding from this exercise: we were not over-complicating the *architecture* in recent turns so much as designing it bottom-up — Aim 2 and Aim 3 first, without Aim 1 in frame. The current text's own preamble already contains the top of the story ("concentrate recruitment in twelve markets that differ in family demand"); what it lacks is the pipeline framing, the marketplace named as one system, and the six changes above. The converged design and the existing text are closer than either of us assumed.
