# The Approach Opener: figure + plain prose (2026-08-19)

The two orientation devices that precede line-by-line editing: the figure that opens
the Approach section (`../figures/research-strategy/fig-approach-overview.svg`,
registered in the manifest as proposed) and the plain-language summary the section
opens with. Both use only everyday words — no cohorts, liquidity, evaluability,
readiness states, or cross-side terminology. The rigor sits underneath, in the tasks.

## The three-sentence version (the elevator test)

> Aim 1 proves that families come to Olera and actually get help — and what that
> costs. Aim 2 proves that Olera's three provider tools deliver real results — hires
> made, inquiries generated, clients admitted — and are worth paying for. Aim 3 puts
> real prices on the tools that proved themselves and measures whether providers buy,
> stay, and generate enough revenue to sustain the whole platform — including keeping
> it free for families.

## The Approach-opening paragraphs (candidate prose, ~210 words)

> Olera's platform already reaches families in nearly every U.S. county, but scattered
> national activity cannot prove a local business. The CRP concentrates it. We will
> select the counties where our family traffic and provider base are already strongest
> and grow all three sides of the marketplace there: families seeking care, providers
> serving them, and health-professions students who expand providers' workforce.
>
> Aims 1 and 2 run in parallel in those counties, and each follows the same three
> steps: finish and verify the product, validate it with the people it serves, and
> grow its user base. Aim 1 does this for the free family-facing CareNavigator —
> confirming that families can use it and that aid and care are actually established,
> while measuring what a family costs to acquire and serve. Aim 2 does it for the
> three provider products — Staffing, Visibility, and Conversion — confirming that
> they deliver hires, inquiries, and admissions, and learning what providers would
> pay.
>
> Aim 3 is the test the entire project builds toward: in counties that now have
> enough real activity, the products that proved their value are offered at real
> prices. New providers encounter them as ordinary customers. We measure who buys, at
> what price, who stays, and whether provider revenue exceeds everything the platform
> costs to run — including free family navigation. If it does, Olera exits the award
> self-sustaining, with the economics rebuilt and verified by an independent
> accountant.

## Notes

- The figure and the paragraphs say the same thing in the same order — a reviewer can
  read either first.
- Candidate figure caption: *"The Approach. Aims 1 and 2 each complete, verify,
  validate, and grow one side of the marketplace in the same selected counties;
  Aim 3 tests whether providers will pay enough to sustain it all."*
- Jargon translation used here (for the aims' technical sections to keep): "adequate
  test conditions" → "enough real activity"; "liquidity" → "activity on all three
  sides"; "advancement rule/plausibility screen" → "products that proved their value";
  "cross-side bar" → "revenue vs. all costs, including free family navigation";
  "conversion/paid-entry cohorts" → "new providers as ordinary customers" (the
  two-funnel detail lives in Aim 3's own text, not the opener).
