**Category-level weakness (the important one): every incumbent redistributes the
existing caregiver pool. None creates net-new caregivers, and the pool itself is
9.7M openings short.** CareNavigator's Staffing difference is categorical:
students entering care work through the provider as employer (paid pilot
evidence; CP Table 7 "student pipeline routed through the provider as employer").

## Differentiation, pressure-tested

**The differentiation is structural, not performance.** The section must claim
structure (pricing model, neutrality, integration, attached demand, workforce
creation) and let the CRP test performance. The CP already models this
discipline: "If Olera can deliver lead quality comparable to incumbent
aggregators... this will represent a significant disruption" — conditional, not
claimed. Never assert performance parity with incumbents; that is Aim 2/3's job.

Survives scrutiny: (1) family demand arrives with the platform and introductions
are free (no per-lead/per-placement toll); (2) flat pricing vs per-placement
economics; (3) neutrality vs paying-partner steering (now externally citable);
(4) workforce creation vs redistribution (students; provider as employer);
(5) conversion tools work on any lead source; (6) one platform across the three
jobs that today require three vendor categories. Rejected: "only platform"
absolutes (phrase structurally: no incumbent category does all three); "free or
lower-friction access" as vague (specify: introductions carry no fee); any
implication that Visibility outperforms Google/APFM on cost per lead (untested;
that is the research).

## The simplest argument (2-3 sentences, pre-prose)

Providers already buy solutions to these problems, one vendor category per
problem: referral marketplaces that charge thousands per placement and recommend
only paying partners, staffing intermediaries that redistribute a caregiver pool
that is 9.7 million openings short, and generic advertising, reputation, and CRM
tools that bring no family demand with them. CareNavigator's Provider Tools do
the same three jobs on one platform where family demand already arrives:
introductions free, pricing flat, recommendations unchanged by payment, and a
student pipeline that adds caregivers instead of reshuffling them. Whether that
structure wins a share of the spending is what the CRP measures.
