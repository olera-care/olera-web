## 1. The hypotheses (each answerable yes or no by award end)

**Central hypothesis.** A free and open eldercare marketplace, where families pay
nothing and no one pays for referrals, can be commercially sustained by providers
buying premium products whose value the marketplace itself creates.

- **H1. Establishment (product).** With execution and follow-up added to
  identification, the system reliably carries families from recognized need to
  established aid and care, confirmed operationally from platform records. [Aim 1]
  Yes/no: establishment rate in operated markets against a pre-registered bar, with
  expert-verified accuracy.
- **H2. Family economics.** Families can be recruited into a local market at an
  acquisition and service cost the provider-funded model can carry. [Aim 1] Yes/no:
  measured CAC and cost to serve against the Aim 3 revenue line.
- **H3. Provider value.** Funding-screened family demand plus responsive
  connections produce measurable provider value: connections complete (response
  rate, response time, care established). [Aim 2] Yes/no against pre-registered
  completion bars.
- **H4. New capacity.** Health-professions students, recruited by Olera and hired,
  trained, and supervised by licensed providers, add caregiving capacity that lets
  providers accept cases they would otherwise decline. [Aim 2] Yes/no: placements,
  semester retention, provider renewals.
- **H5. Willingness to pay.** Providers will pay for Managed Ads and Staff
  Recruitment, and willingness to pay rises with local marketplace activity.
  [Aims 2-3] Yes/no: WTP measured at defined activity levels; sign of the gradient.
- **H6. Self-funding markets.** In at least a pre-declared number of the twelve
  markets, recurring provider revenue exceeds the cost to acquire and serve that
  market's families and providers, verified by an independent financial rebuild.
  [Aim 3] Yes/no per market.

Concentration in twelve markets is the shared testbed, not a hypothesis (ratified
2026-08-21). A negative on H5 or H6 is decision-grade knowledge with pre-committed
alternatives: the plan is designed to disprove, not decorate.

## 2. Research Strategy map

Each section: must answer / load-bearing claims (tagged to hypotheses) / handoff.

**Significance: the unmet need.** Must answer: is the problem important, worsening,
and commercially meaningful? Claims: (1) families fail between identifying care and
establishing it, a pathway with named steps and compounding clinical consequences;
(2) no existing actor is accountable for whether care is established, each role
ends before the pathway does; (3) establishment is local and two-sided: pathways
complete in a county or not at all, and providers themselves cannot say yes
(63.3 percent declined cases for lack of staff). Hands off: a product built to own
that pathway end to end.

**Product and business model.** Must answer: what is the product, and how does its
design attack the problem? Claims: (1) CareNavigator exists: needs screening,
funding qualification, and matching over an expert-curated national database,
validated for usability, in national use; (2) free-and-open is architecture, not
charity: no referral fees keeps every provider visible, removes steering
incentives, and permits the federally reimbursed providers pay-per-referral models
must exclude [central]; (3) revenue comes from the two problems providers already
pay to solve: client acquisition (Managed Ads) and staffing (Staff Recruitment)
[H5]. Hands off: if this design is better, why does no one do it?

**Competitive environment.** Must answer: why is this position empty? Claims:
(1) referral incumbents monetize placement fees, which confines their networks to
private-pay segments and creates the steering families distrust; (2) free and
public tools stop at information, and human navigation is excellent but scarce and
episodic; none executes or follows up; (3) Olera occupies the empty quadrant,
end-to-end establishment plus open network, with a revenue model that does not
collapse the quadrant. Hands off: is the quadrant empty because it is impossible,
or untried?

**Relationship to academic and industry development.** Must answer: what does the
field know, and what has it not done? Claims: (1) federally funded navigation
research validates that establishment support improves outcomes, but none has
produced a self-sustaining deployment; (2) general AI assistants will answer
eldercare questions but stop at answers; our posture is interoperability, the data
and execution layer beneath them, not competition. Hands off: the missing step is
commercialization, which is what the CRP exists to fund; what could still stop
adoption?

**Hurdles to adoption.** Must answer: what could prevent acceptance, and are those
risks measured or merely acknowledged? Claims (post-reframe 2026-08-21): (1) the
binding commercial hurdle is providers adopting and keeping the paid products after
per-lead disappointments elsewhere; mitigation is free entry, measured delivered
value, flat pricing [H5]; (2) families must trust AI-assisted navigation: usability
validated, recommendations grounded in the curated database, and neutrality
preserved because paid products never alter what families see; (3) student
caregiver acceptance is solved structurally by provider employment and supervision
[H4]. Hands off: the hurdles are the hypotheses' failure modes; Innovation says why
we can clear them.

**Innovation.** Must answer: what is genuinely new? Claims: (1) accountable care
establishment: execution and follow-up loops with a confirmed operational endpoint,
where the field stops at information [H1]; (2) new caregiving supply: converting
health-professions students into provider-employed capacity rather than competing
for the existing scarce pool [H4]; (3) business-model innovation: an open
marketplace sustained by provider products with referral fees structurally
excluded, which also unlocks the federally reimbursed majority of care [central,
H5]. (Ordering delegated to TJ.) Hands off: evidence any of this already works.

**Preliminary Work / CRP Progress Report.** Must answer: why believe this team and
platform can produce the answers? Claims: (1) built and validated across Phases
I-IIB: platform, database, peer-reviewed usability, 15,500+ monthly organic
visitors, 725+ onboarded providers; the remaining IIB year completes the agent
layer and the CRP re-funds none of it; (2) each hypothesis already has a real
signal: the identification-to-establishment fall-off measured on our own platform
[H1], a paid staffing pilot with franchise operators [H4, H5], a managed-ads cohort
operating with named budgets [H5]; (3) maturity labeled honestly: live, partial,
proposed. Hands off: the aims are precisely the unknowns this evidence cannot yet
answer.

**Approach / Specific Aims.** Must answer: will the proposed work answer the
hypotheses with decision rules, not just activity? Claims: (1) twelve county
markets are the testbed where both sides concentrate enough for establishment and
willingness to pay to be measurable at all; (2) Aim 1 builds and validates the
establishment loop and measures family economics [H1, H2]; Aim 2 establishes
provider capacity and the two products and measures connection completion,
capacity added, and WTP against activity [H3, H4, H5]; Aim 3 prices under real
billing and independently verifies the self-funding condition [H5, H6]; (3) every
milestone carries a pre-registered bar and a pre-committed alternative, and tools
and markets advance to pricing only where willingness to pay exceeds cost to
serve. Hands off: playbooks, WTP curves, and verified unit economics become the
CP's inputs.

## 3. Commercialization Plan map

**Statement of Need (Valley of Death).** What gap remains and why NIH now: demand
is proven but diffuse; the revenue model is untested; investors fund evidence, not
hypotheses, and the evidence requires operating concentrated markets for roughly
three years, which a pre-revenue company cannot finance. That circularity is the
valley; the CRP is the bridge built for exactly it.

**Value, outcomes, impact.** The award-end asset: a verified, repeatable market
playbook plus a national free navigation utility. Commercial growth and public
benefit are the same event: a paying market is a market where families get care
established.

**Company.** A lean virtual team that has delivered five award phases; all IP
owned; zero dilution; management functions grow with revenue.

**Commercialization history.** Honest pre-revenue record; the transition to
revenue is the CRP's explicit purpose.

**Market, customer, competition.** ~165K provider organizations in the serviceable
market, 72K+ already indexed; incumbents prove providers pay thousands per client,
so category willingness to pay exists; the wedge redirects existing spend to a
model providers prefer: flat, no gatekeeping [H5].

**Competitive advantage.** The directory, telemetry, and neutrality position
compounds; referral-fee incumbents cannot follow into federally reimbursed care
without abandoning their model. The advantage is structural, not feature-based.

**Business model / revenue.** Families free and connections free, always; two
provider products priced as subscriptions and placements; the county is the
economic unit (enter, serve, mature); every CP number that matters is an Aim 3
measurement and is stated as hypothesis until then.

**Milestones (five-year, market-based).** Y1: loops built, first market wave open.
Y2: full acquisition, completion bars met, first products at readiness endpoints.
Y3: real-billing pricing, on the order of 300 paying providers, self-funding
verified where it holds, investor package delivered. Y4-5 (non-SBIR): expansion on
the validated playbook via earnings and/or private capital.

**Go-to-market.** Direct sales in operated counties; national self-serve
conversion of the claimed-provider base; enterprise and franchise partnerships
after local proof.

**Finance and Fundraising Plan.** Dual-scenario honesty answering both prongs of
the NIH criterion: the verified model can grow on its own earnings (no third-party
dependence), and private capital accelerates it. Evidence: David Qu as an engaged
investor shaping the milestones with a letter of support; continuing dialogue with
age-tech investors; provider letters with budgeted amounts. The investor evidence
package answers the four questions investors have told us they need answered.

**IP and regulatory.** Trade secrets, data, contracts, and brand rather than
patent theater. Regulatory posture is an asset: no referral fees avoids the
steering and kickback exposure per-placement models carry in federally reimbursed
segments; HIPAA-aligned data practices; no FDA pathway required.

## 4. The causal chain

1. National organic demand exists but is diffuse (15,500+ monthly visitors at
   near-zero cost).
2. The CRP concentrates families into twelve county markets (Aim 1 acquisition,
   measured CAC).
3. The completed navigation loop carries them from recognized need to established
   aid and care (Aim 1: H1).
4. Establishment activity creates provider value: funded, qualified families
   arriving, and students staffing the shifts that let providers say yes (Aim 2:
   H3, H4).
5. Providers buy Managed Ads and Staff Recruitment, and pay more readily as their
   market grows more active (Aims 2-3: H5).
6. In verified markets, recurring provider revenue exceeds the cost to acquire and
   serve that market (Aim 3: H6).
7. The playbook repeats: earnings open new markets, the evidence package unlocks
   private capital to accelerate, and navigation stays free for families.

## 5. The skeptic's test

**If the CRP succeeds, in three years we will know, with independently verified
numbers, six things nobody knows today:** whether AI-agent navigation can complete
care establishment rather than merely inform it, and at what rate; what a family
costs to acquire and serve in a concentrated market; whether marketplace activity
drives provider willingness to pay, and at what prices; whether students are a
real new source of caregiving capacity that providers pay for; whether any county
reaches the self-funding condition; and therefore whether an open, referral-free
marketplace is a viable business model for eldercare navigation, or whether paid
referrals are the only model that survives. A clean no is also an answer the field
currently lacks, and the plan pre-commits its alternatives.

**Why that answer is worth NIH funding now** rests on three facts that only work
together. The public stake: if the answer is yes, the country gets an eldercare
navigation utility free to every family, including the federally reimbursed
majority of care that referral-fee businesses structurally exclude; no incumbent
will run this experiment, because a yes destroys their model. The financing gap:
the answer requires operating concentrated two-sided markets for about three years
before revenue, which pre-revenue companies cannot fund and investors will not
fund until the answer exists; that circularity is the Valley of Death the CRP was
created for. The sunk science: NIH already built and validated the identification
half across Phases I-IIB; the CRP is the designed final mile, and without it both
the asset and the answer dissipate.

## 6. Flags (where the 2026-08-22 framing and the record diverge)

1. **Two paid products vs three (the largest live inconsistency).** Logan's
   framing, the as-sent Aims, and the 2026-08-21 meeting all carry two paid
   products. The CP still sells three (Staffing, Visibility Boost, Conversion) and
   README section 5 fixes "exactly three." Proposed resolution for ratification:
   the CRP tests two paid products; conversion and follow-up capability is part of
   the free establishment loop (Aim 1), not a third SKU; CP and README reconciled
   in one pass. Until then this is the first inconsistency a careful reader finds.
2. **"Volunteer programs"** in the family-side description has no evidence-ledger
   entry; use "aid, community programs, and lower-cost alternatives" unless
   volunteer routing can be substantiated.
3. **Sustainment tail on H1.** The Drive Aims ask "can that support be
   sustained?"; keep sustainment operational (re-engagement telemetry), never a
   clinical outcome, per the Qiping boundary. H1 above is worded to respect that.
4. **Twelve vs eighteen markets** and the award-end revenue figures remain
   provisional (README section 7); the chain uses twelve; the CP's paid-first
   expansion markets need one reconciliation pass after the Aim 3 redesign.
5. **"National family traction"** is stated as visitors and organic demand, never
   "families seeking care" (evidence lock).
6. Nothing else in the 2026-08-22 framing conflicts: the identification vs
   establishment spine, the three-legged openness rationale, and the front-door
   commercial logic all carry through intact.
