Why not traditional Medicare?

No company is on the hook for a member's total costs: the government pays each bill separately, so there is no buyer to call. The exception is CMS itself, which already pays 390 organizations for dementia navigation (GUIDE, 2024). Traditional Medicare enters through federal programs like that, not through sales.

Why not commercial insurance?

Commercial plans mostly cover people under 65, so the older adult is usually not their member: the working adult child is. The opportunity is therefore an employer caregiver-support benefit for those adult children (monthly fee per employee; comps: Wellthy, Homethrive). A later horizon, sold through benefits brokers.

The rule that never changes

Every buyer above pays on the family's side of the market, for navigation and staffing. No provider ever pays to be listed or to receive a family. Neutrality is never for sale.

What this teaches: the question is not whether institutions pay for navigation (CMS already does); it is whether the evidence exists, and the CRP is the machine that makes it. Only the top row is tested inside the CRP; every other row is what the top row's evidence unlocks.

Free for everyone, always: so why would anyone pay?

A plan could just tell members "use this free site," and some would. We still win: more families, more establishment, more provider value, at near-zero cost to us. What sponsors buy is not access. It is accountable completion for a defined population: outreach to the members they flag, a team that carries every case to the end, verification, and documentation their auditors and quality programs require. Self-service gets a plan some starts and no proof; population programs live on completion rates and paperwork. Free is the front door; the paid product is done-for-you, with receipts.

What this teaches: the member experiences the same free product as every other family; the sponsor experiences a reporting and accountability wrapper around it. The wrapper is cheap to build compared to the loop, which is why the loop is the CRP and the wrapper is the horizon.

4. The expanded conception

One figure, no comparison: what the bigger frame claims, what it changes, and what it must never touch.

Exists today, with evidenceThe CRP builds and tests thisPost-CRP, evidence-gated

Olera is the system that establishes care and proves it. Families use it free, always. Care providers pay for the staffing that lets them say yes. And every verified establishment builds the evidence for the buyers with the deepest pockets: the insurers and health systems that end up paying for the ER visits, hospital stays, and nursing-home placements that follow when care is never established.

The timeline

The customer for the next three years

Care providers, unchanged by the expansion. They buy Staff Recruitment (flat monthly fee vs fee per placement: itself an Aim 3 experiment). Insurers and health systems appear nowhere in the tested aims.

What the award must prove

Outcome: how often care gets established, how fast, and how much aid money reaches families. Economic value: what a family costs to acquire and serve, what providers pay, and whether a market covers its own costs. Replicability: a market-entry playbook, and which market features predicted success.

What happens after the award

Insurers and health systems sign per-case pilots on that evidence; the first pilot doubles as the outcomes study, run on the payer's own data and dime. Markets that cover their costs expand on earnings. Navigation stays free for every family.

The judgment

What the expansion gains

It answers "why does this matter beyond providers." It makes the evidence package itself the product. It gives the vision a national ending, and it speaks the language of the investors and buyers who come next: outcomes, economic value, replicability.

What it risks, and the guard

If insurers or clinical outcomes leak into the tested aims: unfundable scope, a heavier human-subjects burden, lost credibility. The guard is one rule: if we can measure it from our own records or from what participants tell us, it is in the CRP; if it needs insurance claims data or comparison groups, it waits for a payer partner.

What this teaches: the expansion changes the story and the audience, not the experiment. Any sentence about insurers that would need new data to defend belongs after the award, not in it.
