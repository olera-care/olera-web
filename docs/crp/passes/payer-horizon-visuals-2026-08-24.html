1. What is CareNavigator in the eldercare ecosystem?

CareNavigator is the coordination layer of the eldercare ecosystem. It connects a family's needs to the programs and insurance that can pay, and to the organizations that deliver care, and it confirms that care actually begins.

Older adults and families

Most need several kinds of support at once and do not know what they qualify for.

⇅Families arrive on their own or by referral. Everything is free for them.

Public aid and benefit programs

Federal, state, and local programs for food, housing, income, and long-term care.

⇆Determines eligibility and files the applications.

CareNavigator by Olera

It assesses needs, finds what can pay, matches providers, and confirms care began.

⇆Takes referrals when patients need support at home.

Healthcare organizations

Hospitals, health systems, clinics, and ACOs. They identify needs and hold cost risk.

Insurance and coverage

Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid plans, and long-term care insurance.

⇆Finds unused coverage and benefits.
⇆Matches each family to a provider with capacity.

Care and support providers

Home care, home health, hospice, assisted living, nursing, rehab, and adult day care.

How the ecosystem works today
Public programs and insurers pay for most eldercare.
Providers deliver the actual services and support.
Families cover the rest out of pocket.
Nobody coordinates across all of it. That is the gap CareNavigator fills.

3. Who benefits, who pays, and why?

Who would pay Olera, why they would, and what they would actually be buying.

Who they areWhat they get from usWhy it saves them moneyWhat they buyHow they payWhat proof they need first
Care providers. Home care, home health, assisted living, and other care businesses. Families arrive with funding worked out and choose them freely. Finding clients and staff decides their survival. They already pay for both. Recruiting help for caregiver staff and tools to grow their business. A monthly fee or a fee per hire. We will test both. Mostly in hand. Providers paid us in our pilot.
Medicare Advantage plans. Private insurers paid a fixed monthly amount per member. Members who need support at home actually get it, with proof returned. Every prevented hospital stay is money the plan keeps. Helped members stay enrolled. Outreach to members they flag, casework to completion, and results reported back. A fee for each completed case. How often care gets established, how fast, and at what cost per case.
Medicaid managed care companies. Companies states hire to run Medicaid for a fixed amount per person. Waiver applications get finished and members get home care sooner. A nursing home costs about three times more than home care. The same outreach and casework program for waiver-eligible members. A fee for each completed case. The same results, plus patience. Public contracts take one to two years.
Hospitals, health systems, and ACOs. ACOs are paid partly for keeping total costs down. Patients leave on time to home support that actually starts. Waiting patients cost money every day. Readmissions bring penalties. Discharge referrals worked to completion, with closure reported back. A fee for each discharge case. Our completion rate and speed in their counties.
Government aging agencies. State and regional offices running eldercare helplines. Their callers get the follow-through they cannot staff. This serves their public mission more than their budget. Casework capacity behind their existing helplines. Public contracts and grants. The same results. Government buying moves slowly.

Why would any of them pay when the website is free?

The free website serves families who come looking. An institution's problem is the members who never look. What an institution buys is a program built on top of the free platform. We find and contact the specific people they are responsible for, work every case to completion with dedicated staff, and report each result back into their systems with the family's consent. The family's experience stays free and identical either way. This is a proven kind of purchase. Free helplines and benefit checkers have existed for decades, yet plans and hospitals still pay companies to do outreach, follow-through, and reporting on top of them. What remains unproven is the price they will pay us, and that is a question for after the award.

3b. One sponsored case, from referral to payment

Mrs. Rivera's story shows how a health plan, a family, and Olera work together.

1The plan spots the need. A care manager sees Mrs. Rivera needs help at home after her hospital stay. She refers her with one click.
2The family opens the same free tool. Her daughter gets a text and answers a few questions. Any family can use this for free without any referral.
3The money gets found and filed. The system finds aid the family never knew about. Our team completes the applications and follows up until they are decided.
4A provider says yes. The family picks from every provider in the county and care is scheduled with one that has room to take the case.
5Proof comes back and payment follows. Care begins and we confirm it. The plan receives the record and pays only for the finished case.
