3. Who benefits, who pays, and why?

Who would pay Olera, why they would, and what proof each buyer needs first.

Who they areWhat they get from usWhy it saves them moneyWhat they buyHow they payWhat proof they need first
Care providers. Home care agencies and senior living communities. Families arrive with funding worked out. Student caregivers staff their open shifts. Finding clients and staff decides their survival. They already pay others for both. Staff Recruitment. We recruit student caregivers and the provider hires and supervises them. A monthly fee or a fee per placement. We will test which one works better. Mostly in hand already. Providers paid us for placed students in our pilot.
Medicare Advantage plans. Private insurers paid a fixed monthly amount to cover all of a member's care. Their members get home support set up, with proof returned to the plan. Every prevented hospital stay is money the plan keeps. Satisfied members also stay enrolled. Navigation for members they flag, such as after a hospital stay. A fee for each completed case. Later a flat monthly fee per eligible member. Our measured results. How often care gets established, how fast, and at what cost.
Medicaid managed care companies. Private companies states hire to run Medicaid for a fixed monthly amount per person. Waiver paperwork gets finished, so members get home care instead of waiting for a crisis. A nursing home costs about three times more than home care. Every deferred placement is savings. Navigation for flagged members plus our help growing the local caregiver workforce. A fee for each completed case. The same results package. Public buyers also take one to two years to sign a contract.
Hospitals, health systems, and ACOs. ACOs are provider groups rewarded for keeping total costs down. Patients leave the hospital on time, to home care that actually starts. A patient stuck waiting for home care costs the hospital money every day. Readmissions bring penalties. Help getting discharged patients into care. The family picks from a complete list, so nobody steers. A fee for each discharge case. Our completion rate and speed in the counties they discharge into.
Government aging agencies. State and regional offices that run eldercare helplines today. Their callers finally get the follow-through the agencies cannot staff themselves. This is about their public mission more than savings. Budgets come from grants and contracts. Navigation capacity behind their helplines. Public contracts and grants. The same results package. Government buying moves slowly.

3b. One sponsored case, from referral to payment

Mrs. Rivera's story shows how a health plan, a family, and Olera work together.

1The plan spots the need. A care manager sees Mrs. Rivera needs help at home after her hospital stay. She refers her with one click.
2The family opens the same free tool. Her daughter gets a text and answers a few questions. Any family can use this for free without any referral.
3The money gets found and filed. The system finds aid the family never knew about. Our team completes the applications and follows up until they are decided.
4A provider says yes. The family picks from every provider in the county. A student caregiver fills the open shift so the provider can take the case.
5Proof comes back and payment follows. Care begins and we confirm it. The plan receives the record and pays only for the finished case.
