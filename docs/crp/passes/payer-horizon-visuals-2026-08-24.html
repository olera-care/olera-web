1. The ecosystem CareNavigator coordinates

CareNavigator coordinates the eldercare ecosystem around one family's needs. It connects those needs to the programs and coverage that can pay and to the organizations that deliver care, and it verifies that care actually begins.

Older adults and families

Most manage several concurrent needs and rarely know what they qualify for.

⇅Families arrive directly or by referral. The platform is free to them.

Public aid and benefit programs

Federal, state, and local programs for food, housing, income, transportation, and long-term services and supports.

⇆Determines eligibility and completes applications.

CareNavigator by Olera

Assesses needs, maps eligible funding, coordinates providers, and verifies that care begins.

⇆Receives referrals for patients needing home and community support.

Healthcare organizations

Hospitals, health systems, clinics, and ACOs. They identify needs, coordinate care, and bear financial risk.

Insurance and coverage

Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid plans, commercial insurance, and long-term care insurance.

⇆Identifies unused coverage and plan benefits.
⇆Matches families to providers with verified capacity.

Care and support providers

Home care, home health, hospice, assisted living, skilled nursing, rehabilitation, and adult day services.

One intake regardless of entry point. The family describes the situation once, in plain language, online or with a navigator.

2. Needs assessed across every domain

Food, housing, income, transportation, healthcare, long-term care, and personal care, plus what the family itself can manage.

3. Resources mapped to those needs

The system screens the aid programs, insurance benefits, healthcare services, and local providers relevant to this specific person.

→

4. Care and funding plan created

A written plan. What each benefit or coverage will pay for, which applications and approvals are required, and which choices belong to the family.

↓

8. Follow-up over time

CareNavigator confirms each support actually began, monitors that it continues, and reopens the case when circumstances change.

←

7. Aid and care established

What success looks like for one family.

A $1,800 monthly VA pension approved and paying A Medicare Advantage transportation benefit in use A first geriatric clinic visit completed Home care three mornings a week, begun and confirmed
←

6. CareNavigator executes the plan

Applications filed, documents assembled, approvals tracked, assessments scheduled, providers contacted, and family tasks surfaced at each checkpoint.

←

5. Family reviews and approves

The family sees the whole plan, sets priorities, and decides what to pursue. Nothing proceeds without their approval.

3. Who pays, and why?

Each customer category, what it buys, and whether the opportunity is economically meaningful at the county level.

Who they areWhat they get from usWhy it saves them moneyWhat they buyHow they payProof they need firstPossible revenue per county per year*
Care providers. Home care, home health, assisted living, and other care businesses. Families arrive with funding worked out and choose them freely. Client acquisition and staffing decide their survival. They already pay for both. Caregiver recruiting and business growth tools. Monthly fee or fee per hire. We will test both. Mostly in hand. Providers paid us in our pilot. If 25 providers pay about $200 a month: ~$60K
Medicare Advantage plans. Private insurers paid a fixed monthly amount per member. Members needing home support actually get it, with proof returned. Every prevented hospital stay is money kept. Helped members stay enrolled. Outreach to flagged members, casework to completion, results reported back. Fee per completed case, then per member per month. Establishment rate, time to care, and cost per case. If plans refer 500 cases a year at about $400: ~$200K
Medicaid managed care organizations. Companies states hire to run Medicaid per enrollee. Waiver applications finished. Members reach home care sooner. A nursing home costs about three times more than home care. The same outreach and casework program for waiver-eligible members. Fee per completed case. The same results. Public contracts take one to two years. If 200 waiver cases a year at about $500: ~$100K
Hospitals, health systems, and ACOs. ACOs are paid partly for lowering total cost. Patients discharged on time to home support that starts. Waiting patients cost money daily. Readmissions bring penalties. Discharge referrals worked to completion, closure reported. Fee per discharge case. Completion rate and speed in their counties. If one system refers 300 discharges a year at about $200: ~$60K
Government aging agencies. State and area offices running eldercare helplines. Their callers get follow-through the agencies cannot staff. Serves their statutory mission more than their budget. Casework capacity behind their existing helplines. Public contracts and grants. The same results. Government buying moves slowly. One modest contract: ~$25-50K

*Illustrative assumptions for one mid-sized county, not validated economics. Volumes, capture rates, and prices are all untested. The provider row is the only one the CRP itself will test.

Why pay when the website is free? The free site serves families who come looking. Institutions pay us to find and engage the specific people they are responsible for, finish every case, and prove the results back into their systems.
