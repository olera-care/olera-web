If the CRP proves the inner loop, the outer loop becomes a sales problem. If we chase the outer loop now, we skip the proof that powers it.

5. What the CRP proves vs what it positions

The scope-creep guard: which outcomes are proximal enough to measure in three years, and which belong to the longer-term thesis?

Evidence in hand today

Established across Phases I-IIB and pilots

  • Platform + curated national database; usability validated
  • 15,500+ monthly organic visitors
  • 725+ onboarded providers
  • Paid staffing pilot: providers paid, 20+ placements
  • Identification-to-establishment fall-off measured on our own funnel
  • $58B unclaimed aid; 63.3% of home-care providers declined cases (2023)
  • CMS GUIDE precedent: institutions already pay for navigation

The CRP proves (by award end)

From our own platform records + the IRB study

  • Outcome: establishment rate vs pre-registered bar; time to established care; benefit $ secured; connection completion
  • Economic value: family CAC + cost to serve; provider value delivered; willingness to pay at rising activity; price, retention, churn under real billing; capacity added by students
  • Replicability: the self-funding condition per market; a 12-market entry playbook; which market features predicted success
  • Optional secondary (Qiping's call): caregiver-reported burden, self-report only

Positioned, not proven

Requires payer data, comparison groups, or counterparties

  • Reduced ER visits and hospitalizations
  • Fewer 30-day readmissions
  • Delayed institutionalization; total cost of care
  • Payer revenue at scale (PMPM, shared savings)
  • Employer channel economics
  • Note: the first payer pilot IS the study that tests these, co-funded by the payer whose data can answer them
The bright line: measurable from our own platform records or participant self-report → belongs to the CRP.
Requires claims data or comparison populations → a post-CRP hypothesis, addressed to the payer whose data answers it.
What this teaches: David's triad already maps onto the middle column: outcome = H1, economic value = H2/H3/H5/H6, replicability = H6 + the playbook. We adopt his names without adding a single new endpoint. Nothing in the right column ever migrates left without a payer partner and their data.

6. Current thesis vs expanded conception

Is the broader framing a genuine evolution or grander language? What changes, what it costs, and what must not move.

Current thesis (the CRP as written)

"A free and open marketplace, where no one pays for referrals, can be sustained by providers buying products whose value the marketplace creates."

Customer in 3 years
Care providers (staffing first, growth tools second)
Award-end proof
Establishment works, at known cost; providers pay; markets self-fund; playbook replicates
Strength
Falsifiable, self-contained, every endpoint under our control
Weakness
Can read as "just a marketplace"; undersells who else eventually pays and why the record matters

Expanded conception (same experiment, bigger frame)

"The system that establishes care and proves it. Verified establishment creates value for families, providers, and the organizations financially responsible for what happens when care is NOT established."

Customer in 3 years
Unchanged: care providers. Institutions appear only as the evidence-gated second horizon
Award-end proof
Same endpoints, told as outcome / economic value / replicability, plus time-to-care on the scoreboard
What it gains
Answers "why does this matter beyond providers"; makes the evidence package the product; hands the vision a national ending
What it risks
If payers or clinical outcomes leak into the tested aims: unfundable scope, human-subjects reweighting, credibility loss

Adopt (cheap, real gains)

Outcome / economic value / replicability as the award-end frame • time to established care as a headline Aim 1 metric • one payer-horizon passage in the CP (demand-side sponsor logic, GUIDE precedent, case-rate entry) • data-asset and employer sentences in the CP

Keep unchanged (the spine)

H1-H6 and the three aims • twelve markets • provider-funded self-sufficiency as THE tested hypothesis • the neutrality rule • establishment as operational telemetry, never a clinical endpoint

Reject for this CRP

Clinical utilization outcomes as tested endpoints • payer revenue as a tested hypothesis • "operating system" language in NIH prose • any new SKU or market count born from excitement rather than evidence
The answer to the governing question: we are building a better eldercare marketplace whose completion records make it coordination infrastructure over time. The marketplace is how; verified establishment is what; the payers are when-the-evidence-exists. The larger vision belongs in this CRP exactly twice: one paragraph of horizon in the CP, and one metric (time to care) in Aim 1. Everything else it earns later.
