Provider Flowmap

We first build enough family, provider, and student activity in selected counties for the products to work. In Aim 2, we make the products available free and determine whether they actually create provider value — using real-world platform data plus a smaller research cohort that tells us about usability, perceived value, and willingness to pay. Products that demonstrate enough value and plausible economics move into Aim 3, where new providers encounter real prices. We then measure whether they actually buy, keep using the products, receive value, and generate sustainable economics.

Flags raised by drawing it

1 · The marketplace lane crosses aim boundaries. Aim 1 builds the family side; Aim 2 builds the provider and student sides — but they are one marketplace. The RS prose must cross-reference this cleanly so a reviewer sees one infrastructure, not an acquisition project bolted beside the research.

2 · Conversion's evidence window is structurally short. Built during Year 1, dependent on Year-2 demand density, judged at mid-Year 2 — it will likely reach the gate with less evidence than the other two. The proposal should present this as deliberate sequencing (build → verify → validate → price later, with the second round as its natural entry), not as a product falling behind.

3 · The gate reads two lanes but changes only one. Worth stating in the prose exactly as the diagram shows it: the decision changes whether a product carries a price — never whether its value keeps being measured. That single sentence prevents the "what happens to failed products?" reviewer question entirely.
