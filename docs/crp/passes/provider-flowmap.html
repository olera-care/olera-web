Provider Flowmap

We recruit providers in twelve counties, give them the Growth Suite free, and observe whether each product creates real operational value — a hire, a qualified inquiry, an admission. That evidence decides which products are ready to be priced. Then we test real payment two ways: with the providers who already used the products, and with brand-new providers who meet the finished offering cold. By the award's end we know not just whether the products work, but whether we can repeatedly acquire customers, convert them to paid, retain them, and run the model sustainably.

The one-minute picture

Three populations, deliberately different

Your three-population intuition is correct, with one refinement in the third.

1 · The commercial population

Everyone: the ≈725 claimed providers (+≈150/month) nationally, plus everyone acquired during the award. They claim profiles, use free products, and generate operational data. No consent, no IRB — this is a company operating.

2 · Aim 2 research participants

The consented subset: ≈80 provider accounts in the 12 counties (staff are the human subjects) and ≈100 students. Enrolled specifically to answer whether the products create measurable value.

3 · Aim 3 accounts — two layers

All ≈200 billed accounts contribute commercial data (conversion, revenue, churn) — that's selling, not research. A consented subset additionally joins the research layer: surveys, interviews, and any analysis linking billing records to individual people runs under IRB. This dual layer is already in Qiping's design; the flowmap just makes it visible.

The two aims in plain English

Aim 2 — does it create value?

  1. We recruit ≈80 provider organizations in the 12 counties — from our claimed base there, and from new outreach. Roughly 65 have never used the products; about 15 are existing users, capped so enthusiasts can't tilt the result.
  2. Each must be licensed, have a claimed profile, name the staff who'll use the tools, and consent to research.
  3. They get the whole Growth Suite, free.
  4. They use it naturally for at least six months — we never assign products; we record which ones each provider was eligible for and actually used.
  5. The platform logs everything automatically: applicants, hires, inquiries, leads, admissions, time spent.
  6. For Staffing, success is a hire who works a first verified paid shift.
  7. For Visibility, success is qualified family inquiries arriving because of it.
  8. For Conversion, success is an admission produced from a tracked lead.
  9. We also survey and interview the staff and students — including providers who stopped using the products, so we hear the failures.
  10. At mid-Year 2 we ask, per product: did it show enough value to justify testing whether providers will pay? Yes → it gets priced. Not yet → stays free, keeps improving, retested later. No → its parts fold into the other products.

Aim 3 — will they pay for it?

  1. The products that showed value become a real offering: a bundle plus individual subscriptions, at candidate prices drawn from a pricing survey of ~120 provider decision-makers.
  2. Different counties get different price points — so neighbors comparing notes don't contaminate the test.
  3. The Aim 2 providers get the paid offer (~60–70 of them): do providers who know the value pay for it?
  4. ≈130 brand-new providers — mostly in ~6 new counties — get the paid offer from their first contact: can the finished offering win a normal customer who never got months of free use? Without this group we'd only ever know whether free trials convert.
  5. Every account is followed for 12 months of real billing.
  6. We measure: who converts, at what price, who stays, who cancels and why, revenue per account, what each account costs to win and serve.
  7. Interviews explain the numbers — non-payers, cancellers, and loyal accounts all sampled.
  8. A second pricing round is planned in advance: repackage what underperforms, and price late-maturing products that earned a "not yet" in Aim 2.
  9. An outside CPA rebuilds the entire economic model from raw billing records — so investors aren't taking our word for it.
  10. Success is defined before we look: accounts pay back their acquisition cost fast enough, stay long enough, and county-level revenue clears county-level cost — the sustainability the Commercialization Plan promises at award end.

The fourteen questions, answered directly

1 · What role does the existing ≈725-provider base play?
Three roles: its in-county members are Aim 2's first recruitment pool; nationally it keeps growing as the ordinary commercial population; and later it is the base the self-serve paid conversion runs on. It is never wholesale "the study."
2 · Are the ~80 recruited from that base?
Partly. From the base's in-county members, plus providers newly acquired in-county by Task 2.3 outreach. Qiping's text already says both.
3 · Only from Aim 2 counties, or nationally?
The field test: 12 counties only — the products depend on local family demand and campus pipelines. The small usability study can recruit nationally (remote sessions).
4 · Are some already users of the products?
Yes — the ~15 Growth Suite signups, Ad Boost customers, pilot staffing providers. About 15 such accounts enroll, valuable for continuity and their instrumented history.
5 · Mostly never-users on purpose?
Yes: ~80% never-users, prior users capped near 20% and always reported separately, so enthusiasts can't inflate the value estimates.
6 · Where do other acquired providers go?
They're customers, not subjects: free commercial use, operational data only. Many later receive the Aim 3 paid offer as ordinary commerce.
7 · What happens to the ~80 when Aim 2 ends?
They keep their products; research follow-up ends; they receive the real paid offer.
8 · Which are invited into Aim 3?
All still-active accounts — ~60–70 after normal attrition. Their conversion behavior is itself a finding.
9 · Does Aim 3 recruit completely new providers?
Yes — the new-customer cohort, ≈130, paid terms from first contact. Without them we would only ever learn whether months of free product convert — not whether the finished offering can win a normal customer. That is the entire reason the cohort exists.
10 · Of ~200 Aim 3 accounts, how many from each source?
~60–70 continuing + ~130 new.
11 · Where do the new ones come from?
Sales channels (outreach, brands and associations, paid digital) primarily in the ~6 new counties, plus late arrivals in the original 12 — a detail to finalize with Qiping.
12 · How do 12 + ~6 counties fit the flow?
Validate across 12 → monetize in those same 12 and enter ~6 new counties paid-first. Your suggested picture is what the CP §7 and the RS cohorts already intend; it had simply never been drawn.
13 · Is a market always a county?
Yes (CP: "one market is one county"). Recommendation: say county everywhere and retire "market" as a noun; the RS currently never defines it.
14 · Are acquisition, enrollment, activation, and paid conversion four different things?
Yes — four distinct recorded events: acquired (becomes a customer), enrolled (consents to research; subset only), activated (starts using products), converted (pays; Aim 3 only). The diagram's bottom line shows them in order.

Deliberately not locked yet

On the three readiness labels: the underlying decision is one plain question — did this product demonstrate enough provider value to justify testing whether providers will pay for it? — with three possible answers: yes, not yet, no. My recommendation: keep the three answers (they are load-bearing: each has a different Aim 3 consequence) but drop the capitalized state names from the narrative; the protocol can formalize them later if the IRB or reviewers need labels. If "not yet" and "no" ever collapse into one answer in practice, the design loses nothing — simplify then.

Numbers still open by design — flagged TBD above: the value floors per product, the minimum-exposure rules, the usability-study grid arithmetic (currently doesn't close), the count of viable counties required for award-level success, and the whole reconciliation chain counties → providers acquired → research accounts → product users → paying providers → revenue → award-end sustainability, which must resolve into one set of connected numbers across the Aims page, this study, and the Commercialization Plan once Aim 3's design is ratified. None of these inherits from earlier sketches; each gets set with Qiping and the biostatistician against baseline data.
