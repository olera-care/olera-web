- Commercialization Plan: `3. Commercialization Plan`, Drive id

---

## 1. How well the two documents currently align

They agree on the thesis and diverge on the model.

The shared spine holds. Both documents say the same thing about what is wrong
(a recognized need does not reliably become established care), what Olera is
(navigation plus execution over a curated national database), what changes with
this award (execution and confirmation, not more information), what the unit of
success is (care established, not information delivered), and why staffing has to
be in the same application (navigation fails when nobody can deliver the care).
The Research Strategy's "the unit of success changes from information delivered
to care established" and the Commercialization Plan's "does a recognized need
reach established care?" are the same sentence written twice. That is the
expensive part to align, and it is already aligned.

What has drifted is everything downstream of the thesis: how many markets there
are, what a market is, what is sold, at what price, in which year, for how much
revenue, and against which denominators. The Commercialization Plan settled a
commercial model over the last several weeks. The Research Strategy still carries
the model that preceded it, in places verbatim, and in one instance cites the
Commercialization Plan as the source for a number the Commercialization Plan does
not contain.

A reviewer reading both in sequence would not conclude the team disagrees about
the science. They would conclude the commercial model changed after the Research
Strategy was written and the Research Strategy was not updated. That is a
correctable impression, and it is worth correcting because the CRP is scored in
part on whether the research plan and the commercialization plan are one plan.

## 2. Drifts, in priority order

### Tier 1: genuine contradictions a careful reviewer would catch

**A. Market count and the free-to-paid design. Highest priority.**

- *Research Strategy says*: Aim 2 validates in **two** pilot markets, where
  providers "get both products free for the life of the award." Aim 3
  commercializes in "**eight new paid markets**," opened in two waves of four,
  and states that "every market here is new, so conversion carries no prior-gift
  confound." Total footprint: ten markets, described as such ("The ten-market
  footprint is deliberately small").
- *Commercialization Plan says*: Table 8 shows "~8 free" Staffing markets in CRP
  Year 2 and "~8 paid" in Year 3, and Section 7 says "the CRP applies this
  process across approximately eight markets." The natural reading is that the
  same eight markets are given the product free and then charged.
- *Why it matters*: these are different experiments. If the eight paid markets
  were free first, the Research Strategy's stated defense of Aim 3's internal
  validity collapses, because every provider offered a price has already had the
  product as a gift. If they were not free first, the Commercialization Plan's
  Year 2 row is wrong and the free-validation year happened in two markets, not
  eight. The arithmetic downstream also moves: the $240K annualized run rate is
  8 markets x 10 hires x $250 x 12, which matches Aim 3's eight, so the revenue
  model is probably right and the Year 2 row is probably the error, but that is
  an inference and should be a decision.
- *Recommended direction*: adopt the Research Strategy's design (2 free
  validation markets, 8 new paid markets, 10 total) and correct the
  Commercialization Plan's Table 8 Year 2 row to "~2 free" with the eight paid
  markets shown as new in Year 3. Then state the ten-market footprint in both.

**B. Pricing structure: subscription vs per successful hire.**

- *Research Strategy says*: Table 1 is built entirely on "Olera at **$275/mo,
  unlimited hiring**," with effective cost per hire from $293 down to $44 and an
  annual saving column. Task 3.1 sets the starting price range from "prices
  providers have already paid us, about $275 per month and $150 per placement."
- *Commercialization Plan says*: "The Staffing model is intentionally simple:
  successful hires, times realized revenue per successful hire, times active
  county markets," at "$250 per successful hire" with $150 to $350 sensitivity.
  There is no subscription anywhere in the document.
- *Why it matters*: these are not two prices, they are two business models with
  different unit economics, different churn behavior, and different experimental
  designs. Aim 3 assigns four candidate prices across eight markets. If the
  candidates span both structures, the design becomes a two-by-something and is
  underpowered at eight units of assignment. This is the drift most likely to
  draw a reviewer comment, because Research Strategy Table 1 is a full table
  arguing for a model the Commercialization Plan abandoned.
- *Recommended direction*: a business decision, not an editorial one. See
  Section 4.

**C. Revenue per market: $165,000 vs $30,000, with the wrong document cited.**

- *Research Strategy says*: "The Commercialization Plan models both; at modeled
  steady state a single market carries about 50 paying provider accounts and
  **$165,000 in annual recurring revenue** against roughly **$30,000 to enter
  it**, with lifetime value above four times acquisition cost."
- *Commercialization Plan says*: each paid county produces **$30,000** in
  annualized Staffing revenue (10 hires x $250 x 12), and contains no ARR figure,
  no account count, no market entry cost, and no LTV-to-CAC ratio.
- *Why it matters*: three separate problems in one sentence. First, the Research
  Strategy attributes a number to the Commercialization Plan that is not in it.
  Second, $165,000 is exactly 50 accounts x $275 x 12, so it is a derived figure
  from the abandoned subscription model. Third, and worst, the Research Strategy
  says a market costs "roughly $30,000 to enter" while the Commercialization Plan
  says a market yields $30,000 a year. Read together, every market breaks even in
  its first year at best. Neither document intends that.
- *Recommended direction*: delete the commercial modeling sentence from the
  Research Strategy and cross-reference the Commercialization Plan without
  restating its numbers. If a market-entry cost figure is wanted in the Research
  Strategy, it should be the same figure the Commercialization Plan carries, and
  the Commercialization Plan does not currently carry one.

**D. When paid testing starts.**

- *Research Strategy says*: Aim 3 runs "Years 2 to 3," wave one opens at
  **month 21**, wave two at month 30.
- *Commercialization Plan says*: "Paid testing begins in **Year 3**," with Year 2
  as the free validation year and Year 3 producing "six paid-month equivalents."
- *Why it matters*: month 21 is in Year 2. The six-paid-month figure is roughly
  consistent with wave one converting late in Year 2 and wave two at month 30, so
  the money is probably right, but the prose is not. A reviewer checking the
  timetable in Figure 7 against Commercialization Plan Table 8 will find the
  first paid offer in a different award year in each document.
- *Recommended direction*: state months, not years, in both, or state "paid
  offers begin at month 21" in the Commercialization Plan and keep Year 3 as the
  first year carrying material recognized revenue.

**E. Customer discovery conversations: 300 vs 200.**

- *Research Strategy*: "more than 300 customer-discovery conversations with
  owners and operators through NIH and NSF I-Corps," stated twice.
- *Commercialization Plan*: "NSF I-Corps customer discovery (200+ interviews),"
  in Table 3, and "more than 300 I-Corps discovery conversations" does not
  appear.
- *Recommended direction*: pick one, and settle whether NIH I-Corps is included.

**F. Staffing pilot outcome: more than 20 vs 25 placed, and 100 accepted.**

- *Research Strategy*: "drew more than 900 applicants and placed more than 20,"
  stated twice.
- *Commercialization Plan*: "approximately 900 student applications, accepted 100
  candidates, and placed 25 with local providers, with participating students and
  providers returning in a subsequent semester."
- *Recommended direction*: the Commercialization Plan's version is more precise
  and more useful (the 900 to 100 to 25 funnel is the throughput evidence).
  Adopt it in both.

### Tier 2: framing divergence a reviewer could read as two models

**G. The organizing spine: three gates, five steps, or seven steps.**

- *Research Strategy*: Figure 1 and the opening argument are built on **three
  gates** (find the right care, afford it, have someone available to deliver it).
  Later, the Related development efforts paragraph describes a closed loop across
  **five steps** (identify need, determine eligibility and funding, locate
  provider capacity, recruit and place a worker, confirm care began).
- *Commercialization Plan*: Figure 2 and Table 1 are built on a **seven-step
  care-establishment pathway** (Assess Needs, Identify Care, Fund Care, Staff
  Care, Execute Plan, Establish Care, Track Outcomes), and the document calls it
  "the same care-establishment pathway used throughout this application."
- *Why it matters*: this is the intellectual spine of both documents and it is
  currently drawn three different ways across two files. Not a contradiction
  (three gates map onto Identify, Fund, and Staff) but it costs the reader the
  sense that one model is being described. The Commercialization Plan's claim
  that the pathway is used "throughout this application" is currently false at
  the application level.
- *Recommended direction*: the seven-step pathway is the better spine because it
  is the one that names execution and confirmation, which is what the award adds.
  Redraw Research Strategy Figure 1 as the pathway with the vicious cycle as the
  consequence of failure at any step, and retire the five-step enumeration.

**H. Whether Staffing depends on CareNavigator.**

- *Research Strategy says*: "Two things are therefore required at once:
  navigation that runs through to established care, and an available caregiver or
  provider to deliver it. Neither is sufficient alone, and **that is the
  structural claim of this application**."
- *Commercialization Plan says*: Staffing "addresses provider workforce demand
  **regardless of where the underlying client originates**, existing clients,
  externally generated referrals, growth, turnover replacement, or a capacity
  constraint observed through CareNavigator."
- *Why it matters*: both are true and they answer different questions, but only
  one is in each document. The Research Strategy's coupling claim is about care
  establishment. The Commercialization Plan's decoupling claim is about the
  addressable market, and the entire Staffing revenue model rests on it: if
  Staffing could only sell against CareNavigator-generated cases, the revenue
  would be capped by family volume in a county rather than by provider hiring
  demand. The Research Strategy does not contain the decoupling sentence at all,
  so a reviewer reading it alone would size Staffing against CareNavigator case
  volume and conclude the revenue projections are unreachable.
- *Recommended direction*: import one sentence of the decoupling into the
  Research Strategy, next to the structural claim, and keep them explicitly
  distinct: staffing is *necessary* for some care establishment, and *saleable*
  independent of it.

**I. Two different AI strategies.**

- *Research Strategy*: Key Innovation 2 commits to post-training an open-weight
  model on what executed cases returned, so routing improves with volume
  (Figure 5). This is a substantive, defensible innovation claim.
- *Commercialization Plan*: never mentions model post-training. Its AI
  differentiation is the opposite direction of travel: "Olera can expose domain
  data and execution capabilities to search and general-purpose AI interfaces,
  allowing them to become entry points to CareNavigator rather than substitutes."
- *Why it matters*: not contradictory, but they are two different bets and
  neither document acknowledges the other. The Commercialization Plan is arguing
  the data asset is a moat against general AI; the Research Strategy is arguing
  it is training data for a better model. A reviewer asking "what is the AI
  strategy" gets different answers.
- *Recommended direction*: keep both, but state them together in one place. The
  interoperability point belongs in the Commercialization Plan and the
  post-training point in the Research Strategy, each with one sentence
  acknowledging the other.

**J. Workforce denominator: 970,000 a year vs 760,500 a year.**

- *Research Strategy*: PHI, 9.7 million direct-care openings 2024 to 2034,
  roughly 970,000 positions filled a year, used to derive a $4 billion annual
  replacement spend.
- *Commercialization Plan*: BLS, roughly 760,500 home health and personal care
  aide openings a year, 2025 to 2035, used to claim that 60,000 hires at 500
  counties is about 8% of annual openings.
- *Why it matters*: different series measuring overlapping things (all direct
  care vs aides only). Not an error in either, but the same rhetorical move is
  made against two denominators, and the Commercialization Plan's 8% becomes
  about 6% on the Research Strategy's figure.
- *Recommended direction*: choose one denominator for the whole application and
  footnote the other.

**K. Cost of a hire: $520 vs $2,700.**

- *Research Strategy*: "$520 per hire," the published median caregiver
  acquisition cost through word of mouth, plus 16% of annual pay as replacement
  cost.
- *Commercialization Plan*: "up to about $2,700 per replacement," recruiting and
  training.
- *Why it matters*: both are Activated Insights figures for adjacent concepts,
  five times apart, and neither document distinguishes acquisition cost from
  full replacement cost. A reviewer who notices will not know which one anchors
  the willingness-to-pay argument.
- *Recommended direction*: define both once, in the Commercialization Plan, as
  distinct quantities, and have the Research Strategy use whichever anchors its
  pricing table after decision B is made.

**L. What the Year 3 gate is, and whether we say we would stop.**

- *Research Strategy*: Aim 3 success criteria are per-market profitability
  positive, retention reported, independent validation delivered, evidence
  package delivered. No revenue figure appears. It also carries an explicit stop
  rule: at month 30, if under 40% of wave-one placed workers remain in direct
  care at 90 days and paid conversion is under 20% at every price, wave two is
  held and the effort redirects to publishing. "A commercialization program
  should fund projects willing to find out they are wrong."
- *Commercialization Plan*: Table 9's Year 3 milestone is "About $120K recognized
  Staffing revenue and about $240K annualized exit run rate," in a table whose
  column heading is "Decision or financing gate." The caption says revenue values
  are projections rather than success criteria, which resolves it, but the layout
  works against the caption. No stop rule appears anywhere.
- *Why it matters*: read together, the Research Strategy is willing to fail and
  the Commercialization Plan projects revenue regardless. That asymmetry reads as
  the commercial document overclaiming.
- *Recommended direction*: move the revenue figures out of the gate column in
  Commercialization Plan Table 9 into a separate projection column, and add one
  sentence carrying the Research Strategy's stop rule into the Commercialization
  Plan. It is a credibility asset, not a liability.

**M. What a market is.**

- *Research Strategy*: "market," "local market," "pilot market," "wave," never
  defined. Selection criteria are organic family traffic, provider density, what a
  household can secure under that state's aid rules, and proximity to a
  health-professions campus.
- *Commercialization Plan*: "Olera's geographic unit of commercialization is a
  local market, defined as one U.S. county or county equivalent," with more than
  3,100 nationally. Selection signals are family demand, provider density and
  workforce need, recruitable workforce supply and nearby university or
  community-college infrastructure, existing local relationships, and feasibility
  of concentrating participation.
- *Why it matters*: the county definition is load-bearing for the revenue model
  ($30,000 per county, 500 counties, 3,100 available). The Research Strategy is
  where markets are selected and it never says what one is. The criteria lists
  also differ: the Research Strategy has state aid generosity, which the
  Commercialization Plan drops; the Commercialization Plan has existing local
  relationships, which the Research Strategy drops.
- *Recommended direction*: adopt the county definition in the Research Strategy
  at first use, and reconcile the two criteria lists into one.

**N. Hires per county per month.**

- *Commercialization Plan*: 10 successful hires per county per month is the base
  case, with a stated mature range of 10 to 30. Every revenue figure in the
  document derives from it.
- *Research Strategy*: never states it, although Aim 3 is the experiment that
  measures it. Aim 3 instead sizes on about 40 accounts per market reaching a
  priced offer, 320 in all.
- *Why it matters*: the Research Strategy should name the planning assumption its
  own experiment is designed to test. It also lets the conservatism be shown: at
  75% turnover a 15-caregiver agency needs roughly 11 hires a year, so 40
  accounts in a market plausibly demand far more than 10 hires a month, which
  makes the base case visibly conservative rather than merely asserted.
- *Recommended direction*: state 10 per county per month in the Research Strategy
  as the planning assumption Aim 3 measures, and state the 40-accounts figure in
  the Commercialization Plan so the two sizing bases are visibly compatible.

directly for care navigation through the GUIDE model." The Commercialization Plan
is more precise: GUIDE pays a per-beneficiary-per-month amount for *dementia*
care management, coordination, and caregiver support. Adopt the narrower phrasing.

## 3. Research Strategy sections needing homogenization, and length

Current state: 9,097 words, 7 figures, 6 tables (one of which is missing). The
Commercialization Plan fits 6,340 words, 6 figures and 9 tables into 12 pages.
At the same density the Research Strategy needs to lose roughly **2,700 to 3,000
words, about 30%**. That is achievable almost entirely by deletion and
consolidation, without cutting a single result.

Section word counts:

| Section | Words | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Significance | 1,738 | 19% |
| Innovation | 991 | 11% |
| Approach | 5,226 | 57% |
| CRP Progress Report | 578 | 6% |
| Bibliography | 564 | 6% |

Where the length is, in order of return:

1. **Approach, Aim 1 (2,091 words).** The largest single block. Task 1.3's
   human-subjects methods run 584 words at protocol length (design, participants,
   recruitment, data collection, data management, analysis). The PHS Human
   Subjects form carries consent, privacy, and risk, so the Research Strategy
   needs design, n, measures, and analytic approach only. Roughly 250 words
   recoverable there, another 150 from Task 2.4's 369.
2. **The duplicated evidence base.** 72,000 records, 578 guides, more than 900
   applicants, more than 700 claimed listings, more than 300 I-Corps
   conversations, and the 50-to-500 traffic growth each appear two or three times
   across Significance, Approach ("The system today"), and Progress Report
   Table 6. Consolidating to one home, most naturally Table 6, is worth roughly
   300 words and materially strengthens the argument by not repeating itself.
3. **Significance, market and competition (384 words plus Table 1).** This
   duplicates Commercialization Plan Section 4 wholesale, and Table 1 encodes the
   pricing model that is under review. The Research Strategy does not need to
   re-argue the market; a short cross-reference plus the workforce facts that
   motivate the science is enough. Roughly 300 words plus a full table.
4. **"Related development efforts in academia and industry" (292 words).** One
   undifferentiated block, weakest prose in the document, and most of its claims
   carry no citation marker. Best converted to a four-row table (care
   coordination, home-based intervention, digital caregiver support, workforce
   development; what each established; what remains unsolved). Roughly 170 words
   recovered and a substantial legibility gain.
5. **Figures 6 and 7 overlap.** Figure 6 shows what each stage establishes;
   Figure 7 is the three-year timetable with the same four decision points. One
   figure can carry both. Figures 2 and 4 are also candidates to merge (demand
   rising while supply contracts; existing channels move workers rather than add
   them are two halves of one argument).
6. **The Aim 1 metrics table has 16 rows**, several of which are "Reported" with
   no criterion. Reported-only measures can move into the task prose, leaving the
   table to carry actual gates. Perhaps 6 rows and useful vertical space.
7. **"Potential problems and alternative strategies" paragraphs** are long
   single-paragraph runs. Each can lose about a third without losing a
   contingency.
8. **Team paragraph** duplicates Commercialization Plan Section 3 and the Project
   Management Plan.

On style: Significance, Innovation, and the Aim 1 and Aim 3 task prose are
already close to the target voice and in places better than the Commercialization
Plan. This is not a document that needs rewriting. It needs the three weak
paragraphs repaired, the duplication removed, and the commercial model updated.

## 4. For TJ and Qiping, not to be resolved editorially

1. **Pricing structure (TJ).** Subscription at $275 a month with unlimited
   hiring, or $250 per successful hire? This is the decision everything else
   waits on: Research Strategy Table 1, the $165,000 ARR figure, Aim 3's four
   price arms, and every revenue line in the Commercialization Plan. If both
   structures are to be tested, Aim 3 needs to be designed for it, and eight
   markets will not support crossing structure with price.
2. **Free-then-paid, or new paid markets (TJ, with Qiping on validity).** If the
   eight Aim 3 markets were free in Year 2, the "no prior-gift confound" defense
   has to go and conversion has to be interpreted differently. If they are new,
   the Commercialization Plan's Year 2 row is wrong.
3. **Ten markets or eight (TJ).** The randomization-inference argument is
   explicitly powered on eight units of assignment, and the $240K run rate is
   eight markets. The two free validation markets need to be visible in the
   Commercialization Plan or absent from the Research Strategy.
4. **Whether 10 hires per county per month is compatible with 40 accounts per
   market reaching a priced offer (TJ).** They are probably compatible and
   conservative, but the two documents currently size on different bases and
   nobody has written down the reconciliation.
5. **Whether Aim 2's 400 households in two markets underwrites any county-level
   economic claim (Qiping).** Establishment rate at plus or minus 5 points, yes.
   County economics, no, and the documents should not let the two blur.
6. **The AHRQ Technical Brief 49 percentages (Qiping).** The Commercialization
   Plan's opening evidence anchor (52% vs 34% emergency department use, 36% vs
   24% hospital or rehabilitation stays) could not be verified against the brief
   across four retrieval routes. The Research Strategy makes the parallel claim
   from Hass, DePalma, and a 2024 systematic review. If the AHRQ figures do not
   hold, the Commercialization Plan should move to the Research Strategy's
   sources.
7. **Post-training an open-weight model on executed case data (Qiping and
   counsel).** Key Innovation 2 commits to it. Whether the IRB protocols and the
   consent language cover secondary use of case data for model training is a real
   question and it is easier to answer now than after the protocols are written.
8. **Task numbering for the PHS Human Subjects form (Qiping).** Tasks 1.5 and
   2.4, or 1.3 and 2.4.
9. **The workforce denominator (TJ).** PHI's 970,000 a year or BLS's 760,500.
   Choosing changes a headline claim in the Commercialization Plan.

## 5. Proposed workflow for the homogenization pass

The ordering below is Logan's, with one step added in front of it, because two of
the Tier 1 items are business decisions that cascade into every section and
cannot be resolved by an editor.

**Step 0. Settle the four commercial decisions.** Pricing structure, market count
and the free-to-paid design, the workforce denominator, and David Qu's committed
role. Half an hour with TJ. Everything downstream is mechanical once these are
fixed; nothing downstream is safe before.

**Step 1. Homogenize the concepts.** One spine (the seven-step care-establishment
pathway), one market definition (the county), one vocabulary for products,
layers, workers, providers, and buyers, one statement of the
CareNavigator-to-Staffing relationship carrying both the coupling and the
decoupling. Produce a one-page terminology sheet first and apply it to both
documents, so the Commercialization Plan moves too where it is the one that is
wrong.

**Step 2. Establish internal logical coherence.** Reconcile every shared number
against a single quantitative spine (markets, years, months, hires, price,
revenue, applicants, placements, discovery conversations, database size,
denominators), fix the Tier 4 internal problems, build or recover Table 2, and
verify that each document's cross-references to the other resolve to something
that exists.

**Step 3. Bring the prose into house style.** Repair the three weak paragraphs,
remove grant register where it appears, no em dashes, and keep the Research
Strategy's opening voice, which is one of the best things in either document.

**Step 4. Reduce to 12 pages.** Deletion and consolidation first, per Section 3
above, with the rendered page count verified rather than estimated. Everything
removed gets logged with a future home.

**Step 5. Reassess for genuine scientific and intellectual gaps.** Only here.
The point of steps 1 through 4 is that the remaining weaknesses become visible
once the noise is gone.

Steps 1 and 2 produce a change log for TJ and Qiping to review before step 3
begins, so the conceptual decisions are ratified before any prose is rewritten.
