# The Unified Architecture: build/verify → validate → adopt (2026-08-19)

Pressure-test of Logan's organizing principle against the current RS. Verdict: the
structure is not an imposition on the existing text — it is the existing text's own
skeleton, stated once. The sequencing problem resolves through the three-levels-of-
evidence distinction; geography resolves through "adequate test conditions vs. full
liquidity" plus a tiered county set that the RS's own wave structure already implies.

## 1. Does the RS support the parallel (non-sequential) reading? Yes — explicitly.

- The Approach preamble: "Aims 1 and 2 run concurrently."
- The Aims page describes Aim 1 as verify (1) + build loops (2) + "integrate the
  system and accelerate Build-Measure-Learn in twelve beachhead markets through
  active family acquisition" (3) — all inside Years 1–2, not in sequence.
- Table 1's Year-1 row: "Aims 1-2 launch … first market wave opens" — the adoption
  machinery starts in Year 1, the same year as verification.
- Task 2.3's waves run across Years 1–2 while 2.2 validates.

So the numbered tasks describe the **logical progression of evidence** (a product
cannot be validated before it is verified; it cannot be priced before it is
validated), while the **calendar runs three parallel streams**: product work,
validation studies, and continuous acquisition. The text intends this but never says
it — a reviewer could misread sequentiality. The fix is one declarative sentence in
the Approach preamble, not a redesign: task numbers order evidence, not time.

**The true dependency graph** (the only hard orderings):
verify(X) → validate(X) → advancement decision(X) → price(X), per product X; and
validate(X) additionally requires *adequate test conditions for X* — which early
acquisition provides. Acquisition itself depends on nothing and starts on day one.

## 2. The three levels of evidence — pressure-tested

Logan's distinction survives, and it is the key that unlocks the sequencing problem:

1. **Verification** — did we build what we intended? No liquidity needed; no humans.
2. **Validation** — do intended users use it, and does it create intended value,
   **under adequate test conditions**? Needs *selected, sufficient* conditions —
   not a mature marketplace.
3. **Adoption / commercial testing** — can we acquire real users at measured cost,
   create marketplace density, and (Aim 3) have providers pay under real conditions?
   This is where full liquidity is both built and required.

Adequate test conditions, per product — and how much already exists:

| Product | Validation needs | Already available? |
|---|---|---|
| Family CareNavigator (Aim 1, n≈25 study) | moderated remote sessions over the live national platform | fully — no local liquidity required at all |
| Staffing | a few provider-campus pairs with real vacancies and student flow | the pilot showed one campus supplies a county; validation needs one to a few activated campus counties |
| Visibility/Boost | family search activity + complete profiles (+ ad budget — external reach is budget-limited, not traffic-limited) | strongest existing counties + modest spend suffice; baseline is already ≈3 leads/provider/month nationally |
| Conversion | providers with live inquiry flow (any source) | such providers exist today (≈2,400 leads/mo across the base); select by existing flow rather than wait for build-out |

**One honest nuance (the only place liquidity touches Aim 2's evidence):** field
value-delivery *rates* are conditional on marketplace conditions — a thin county
understates a product. Handle by reporting value rates alongside the liquidity
present (e.g., leads/provider at the time), treating liquidity as a measured
covariate rather than a gate. Aim 3's cross-side model already treats family demand
exactly this way, so the design is consistent end to end.

**Full local liquidity is therefore:** an *output* of the adoption streams, and a
*precondition* of Aim 3's market-level economics — not a precondition of validation.

## 3. Geography, worked backward (no inherited numbers)

- **The natural liquidity unit is the provider's service area** — the commuting/
  catchment zone families search within, students travel within, and caregivers work
  within. **County is the right operational proxy**: licensing, the database, the SEO
  engine, market selection, and analytics are all county-organized, and a county
  approximates the service area in most of the country. One honest refinement for
  prose: large metros may need county-cluster treatment. So: county — justified by
  service-area logic and data organization, not merely inherited.
- **Where to concentrate:** reinforce strength. Rank counties by existing organic
  family demand, provider density, claimed-base presence, aid generosity, and campus
  proximity — four of these five are already the RS's stated selection criteria, and
  all are pullable from the analytics at county level today. Active acquisition goes
  where passive traction is already strongest; we do not seed deserts.
- **Per-product concentration differs:** Staffing is inherently local
  (campus-county); Visibility's fuel can be *manufactured* locally with ad budget
  (least constrained); Conversion needs per-provider inquiry flow (most
  demand-dependent, but selectable by existing flow during validation). The family
  product is national with only its provider-connection leg local.
- **Validation counties ⊂ build counties.** Validation runs in the few strongest
  counties (which the RS's wave structure already makes wave 1); the adoption streams
  build the full set; Aim 3's pricing runs where density is reached. Tiers = waves —
  already implicit, needs stating.
- **How many counties (derivation drivers, not a number):** the binding scientific
  constraint is Aim 3's pricing experiment — enough matched county-clusters for arm
  assignment (set with the biostatistician); plus demand variation for the cross-side
  hypothesis; bounded by per-county entry cost and ops capacity. Validation alone
  would need only ~4–6. Twelve may well survive re-derivation — but as an output of
  those three drivers, stated as such. The paid-entry count is derived at the same
  time.

## 4. The Approach, cold (refined from Logan's sentences)

> Olera already operates nationally, but national scale is not local liquidity.
> Aim 1 builds, verifies, and validates the family-facing CareNavigator, and its
> adoption work concentrates family demand in selected counties while measuring what
> a family costs to acquire and serve. Aim 2 builds, verifies, and validates the
> three provider products with the users each product's test conditions require, and
> its adoption work concentrates providers and students in the same counties.
> Validation needs only adequate test conditions, which our strongest existing
> counties already provide; the acquisition streams run continuously and build full
> local liquidity. Aim 3 is the convergence: in counties with sufficient density,
> the validated products carry real prices, and we measure whether providers buy,
> stay, and generate economics that sustain the entire system — including the free
> family side.
