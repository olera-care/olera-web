# The research record

Peer-reviewed papers and manuscripts from the Phase I to IIB awards, preserved as
source PDFs. **Levels of evidence are not interchangeable** and are labeled below:
published, manuscript in preparation, completed unpublished analysis, operational
data, production metric.

## Published, peer reviewed

### 1. Fan et al. 2023 — the origin study
`aging_v6i1e47577.pdf` · Fan Q, DuBose L, Ory MG, Lee S, Hoang MN, Vennatt J, Kew CL,
Doyle D, Falohun T. "Financial, Legal, and Functional Challenges of Providing Care
for People Living With Dementia and Needs for a Digital Platform: Interview Study
Among Family Caregivers." **JMIR Aging 2023;6:e47577.** doi:10.2196/47577

*Purpose:* characterize caregiver challenges and needs before designing the platform.
*Sample:* 30 of 150 screened family caregivers; 67% primary caregivers, 93% caregiving
a year or more, 83% over 50, 77% female, 83% White, 73% college educated.
*Methods:* semistructured Zoom interviews Jan to May 2022, follow-up survey, thematic
analysis in a framework approach.
*Findings:* challenges cluster into functional care needs and financial and legal
challenges; participants identified the need for an integrative digital platform.
*Supports:* the "we learned X, therefore we built Z" origin logic for the family
navigation innovation; the Significance claim that families cannot navigate
financial and legal complexity; customer-discovery credibility. **This is the only
study establishing that the product design came from documented caregiver research.**

### 2. Fan et al. 2024 — first platform evaluation (MARS)
`aging_v7i1e55132.pdf` · Fan Q, Hoang MN, DuBose L, Ory MG, Vennatt J, Salha D, Lee S,
Falohun T. "The Olera.care Digital Caregiving Assistance Platform for Dementia
Caregivers: Preliminary Evaluation Study." **JMIR Aging 2024;7:e55132.**

*Sample:* 30 adult caregivers in Texas; 83% over 50, 77% female, 83% White, 67%
financially stable.
*Methods:* two interviews with platform engagement between; adapted Mobile
Application Rating Scale; descriptive statistics and two-sample t tests.
*Findings:* **overall MARS 4.57 (SD 0.57) of 5.** Engagement 4.10, functionality 4.46,
aesthetics 4.58, information quality 4.76. All participants would recommend the app;
90% (27/30) rated it positively overall; 70% favored anonymous interaction before
personalized feedback.
*Supports:* usability and quality of the first-generation platform.
**Correction owed:** the Research Strategy says "4.6 of 5 ... (n=31)" and the Phase IIB
application says "4.6/5 among 25 caregivers." The published values are **4.57 and
n=30**. Neither prior statement matches the paper.

### 3. Hoang et al. 2026 — second platform evaluation (TAS)
`formative-v10-e92967.pdf` · Hoang MN, Kim L, Fisher L, DuBose L, Ory MG, Lee S,
Falohun T, Fan Q. "Exploring Informal Caregivers' Perception of the Olera Digital
Caregiving Assistance Platform for Dementia Care: Mixed Methods Evaluation Study."
**JMIR Formative Research 2026;10:e92967.**

*Sample:* 65 US caregivers, mean age 59.9 (SD 9.8); 95.3% female, 69.2% non-Hispanic
White, 64.6% adult children of the care recipient.
*Methods:* four weeks of platform use, then adapted Technology Acceptance Survey plus
open-ended questions; ANOVA, t tests, linear regression; thematic analysis.
*Findings:* **overall TAS 5.83 (SD 0.85) of 7**, every item above 5.0. Higher use
frequency associated with higher acceptance (F3,61=7.88, P<.001). Also captured
caregiver attitudes toward integrating AI into caregiver services.
*Supports:* acceptance of the second-generation platform after sustained use, not just
first impressions; the dose-response relationship is a genuine strength; caregiver
openness to AI.
**Correction owed:** the Research Strategy says "the AI agents scored approximately
5.6 of 7." The published figure is **5.83 with n=65, and it evaluated the platform,
not the AI agents.**

### 4. DuBose et al. 2024 — the study protocol
`resprot_v13i1e64127.pdf` · DuBose L, Fan Q, Fisher L, Hoang MN, Salha D, Lee S,
Ory MG, Falohun T. "Development and Evaluation of a Web-Based Platform for
Personalized Educational and Professional Assistance for Dementia Caregivers:
Proposal for a Mixed Methods Study." **JMIR Research Protocols 2024;13:e64127.**

*Content:* the registered design. Phase I, at least 25 caregivers, Design Thinking,
adapted MARS. Phase II, 300 dementia caregivers in Texas, deliberately stratified as
100 African American, 100 Hispanic or Latinx, 100 non-Hispanic White, four weeks of
use, TAS. Documents the **Dementia Care Personalization Algorithm**. NIA funding
3 Sep 2021; TAMU IRB 8 Dec 2021; phase I data Jan to May 2022; phase II IRB
21 Jun 2023, recruitment from 1 Jul 2023.
*Supports:* that the evaluation program was pre-registered and peer reviewed rather
than assembled after the fact, and the deliberate racial and ethnic stratification,
which is relevant to NIH inclusion criteria.

## Manuscript in preparation

### 5. CARE-NAV technology acceptance (the n=31 study)
Drive: *CARE-NAV Manuscript 2 Quantitative TAS*. Target JMIR Aging or JMIR Formative
Research. **Unpublished; Logan can substantiate result and manuscript status.**

*Sample:* **N=31** unpaid dementia family caregivers, final analyzed sample. 87%
female; 52% White, 19% Black, 19% Hispanic, 10% other; 39% below $50,000 household
income; mean age 51.0; care recipient mean age 77.1.
*Methods:* moderated Zoom sessions Jan to Apr 2026; pre-survey, hands-on platform use
with think-aloud, semistructured interview, post-survey; modified TAS.
*Findings:* **total TAS 5.73 (SD 1.02) of 7**, median 5.89, IQR 5.21 to 6.52. Content
quality highest at 6.03; ease of use lowest at 5.39.
*What was tested:* per the Year 2 RPPR, the integrated multi-agent eldercare-AI Network
with its voice-to-voice interface, **not** the production benefits finder in olera-web.
*Supports:* the AI navigation innovation, tested with users at the architecture the
CRP proposes to integrate. A companion qualitative manuscript exists.
**Caution:** the Year 2 RPPR reports the Aim 2 pilot as n=25 and states the team is
"intentionally not reporting preliminary quantitative benchmarks before formal
analysis is complete." Cite as unpublished data, manuscript in preparation.

## What the record shows as a whole

Three independent user evaluations across three platform generations, 126 caregivers
evaluated plus 30 interviewed at design stage, with results improving as the product
matured (MARS 4.57 of 5, then TAS 5.83 of 7 after four weeks of use, then TAS 5.73 of
7 on the multi-agent system with voice). Two of the three samples are meaningfully
diverse. That is a stronger and more defensible feasibility record than the current
Research Strategy claims, and it is currently misreported in two places.
