# MOCK NIH SUMMARY STATEMENT — INDEPENDENT REVIEW

**Scope of this mock review:** Specific Aims page only (CareNavigator-only draft, 2 pp.).
**Review posture:** SBIR Commercialization Readiness Pilot (CRP), Special Emphasis Panel.
**Package conditions:** Research Strategy, Commercialization Plan, Human Subjects materials, biosketches, and letters of support were **not** supplied and are assumed competent, internally consistent, and reasonably supportive. They are treated as neutral — neither credited nor penalized. A historical summary statement was supplied as a genre exemplar only; its scores, criticisms, and determinations were quarantined and were not used as priors.

---

## RESUME AND SUMMARY OF DISCUSSION

This CRP application proposes to advance CareNavigator — an eldercare navigation technology developed across prior NIA SBIR Fast-Track and Phase IIB awards — from a system that *identifies* appropriate benefits and services to one that *executes* the administrative work required to obtain them and *verifies* whether aid or care actually began. The applicant frames the residual problem as a "care-establishment gap": recognizing a need and matching it to a resource does not mean the household obtains the resource. Three aims are proposed: (1) engineer and technically verify a longitudinal Care Establishment Model with bounded, permission-gated AI agents, followed by a controlled mixed-methods trust-and-comprehension study; (2) prospectively enroll family care episodes and measure verified establishment of the episode's primary need, alongside time-to-establishment, failure mechanisms, escalation, fidelity, safety, and cost to serve; and (3) convert measured Aim 2 performance into buyer-specific institutional use cases, an evidence package, and a specification of the post-CRP, privately financed proof-of-concept.

Enthusiasm centered on the conceptual framing and the endpoint discipline. Reviewers found the care-establishment gap to be a real, under-addressed, and commercially consequential problem, and considered the applicant's decision to reject proxy endpoints — explicitly stating that a recommendation, referral, submitted application, approval, provider match, or scheduled service does **not** constitute success — to be unusually rigorous for this product category and a meaningful differentiator from the screener/directory/referral field. The staged go/no-go architecture (Aim 1 gates workflow classes into Aim 2; Aim 2 gates advancement to Aim 3) was viewed as properly sequenced. The safety posture visible in the aims — permission-gating of consequential actions, verification of required authority, escalation of ambiguity, and explicit measurement of material error — was noted favorably by reviewers alert to agentic AI acting on households' behalf against government programs.

Concerns were substantial and converged on three issues. First, the aims page contains **no quantitative commitments of any kind**: no sample sizes, no follow-up duration, no success thresholds, no timeline, no baseline or target establishment rate, no market or buyer counts. The word "prespecified" appears repeatedly in place of the specifications themselves, which several reviewers read as criteria that may not yet exist. Second, Aim 2 is described as a single-arm prospective study with **no comparator, benchmark, or historical baseline**, leaving the primary endpoint uninterpretable — a verified-establishment rate cannot be judged good or poor without a reference. Third, and most consequential for a CRP, the **customer is undefined**: the buyer is described only as "organizations responsible for populations with unresolved eldercare needs," and Aim 3 proposes to *discover* who that buyer is, what they would pay, and what evidence they require. Reviewers noted that customer discovery is a Phase I activity, and that a CRP — the last-mile bridge to private capital — is normally expected to de-risk contracting with a known buyer, not to identify the buyer.

Reviewer disagreement is likely on the interpretation of Aim 3. The applicant is explicit and honest that the CRP endpoint "is not assumed institutional ROI" but rather "a buyer-tested deployment and evidence specification." Some reviewers will credit this as refreshing rigor and appropriate scoping. Others will observe that the terminal deliverable of the award is a *specification of the evidence that will later be needed*, and will question whether the promised financing inflection is real or deferred by one more cycle. A related concern is that the aims page carries no commercialization traction whatsoever — no revenue, paying users, institutional relationships, pilots, IP, or prior third-party capital — which leaves Aim 3 reading as though it begins from zero buyer contact.

On balance, the majority view was that the significance of the problem, the quality of the conceptual framing, the rigor of the primary endpoint, and demonstrated continuity through a prior Phase IIB award outweigh the weaknesses — but that the weaknesses are not cosmetic. Two of them (single-arm design without a reference; buyer identity deferred to Aim 3) are design choices visible on this page that a strong Research Strategy or Commercialization Plan could restate but not repair. The remainder are high-cost presentation risks that would be substantially resolved by adding quantitative commitments to this page.

## CRITIQUE 1 — INTEGRATED PRIMARY REVIEWER

### Overall Impact

This is a well-conceived project attacking the correct residual problem in a field crowded with tools that stop at recommendation. The applicant has identified a genuine and defensible wedge — accountability for whether care was actually established — and has committed to an endpoint definition strict enough that a positive result would be commercially meaningful rather than self-congratulatory. Continuity from Fast-Track through Phase IIB, with a family-validated predicate product, materially increases confidence that the team can build what is described.

Enthusiasm is constrained by what the page does not commit to. There are no numbers anywhere in the document: not one sample size, threshold, duration, rate, price, market count, or date. For an application whose central argument is that *measured performance should replace hypothetical claims*, an aims page that contains no measurements undercuts its own thesis. The five invocations of "prespecified" ask reviewers to take on faith the very criteria that constitute the risk-retirement logic. Aim 2's primary endpoint — the scientific core of the application — has no comparator, so a result of any magnitude will be difficult to interpret and harder still to sell to an institutional buyer. And the commercial endpoint is a specification of future evidence rather than evidence itself, obtained from buyers who are not yet named.

The result is a proposal that will likely produce interesting and honest findings but that, as written, does not demonstrate it will produce the specific evidence a third-party investor or an institutional purchaser would require. Impact is Very Good.

### 1. Significance — Score: 3

**Strengths**
- The care-establishment gap is a real, well-articulated, and under-served problem. The distinction between *identifying* a resource and *obtaining* it is stated crisply in the first paragraph and is immediately recognizable to anyone familiar with Medicaid HCBS applications, VA benefits, or home care initiation.
- The framing correctly locates the applicant relative to a crowded field: directories, benefit screeners, referral services, and AI tools all terminate at recommendation. Positioning downstream of that terminus is a defensible strategic choice.
- The problem's drivers are named specifically and accurately — variation by household, program, provider, and geography; incomplete and outdated operational information; delays and denials requiring persistence. This demonstrates real domain understanding rather than generic market-size rhetoric.
- If achieved, the aims would change the accountability standard for the category, from recommendation quality to establishment rate. That is a paradigm-relevant contribution, not merely a product improvement.
- Continuity from prior NIA SBIR awards establishes that the predicate technology exists and that the proposed work is the logical next increment rather than a pivot.

**Weaknesses**
- **The customer, user, and buyer are not distinguished, and the buyer is not named.** "Organizations responsible for populations with unresolved eldercare needs" spans Medicare Advantage plans, Medicaid managed care organizations, ACOs, health systems, employer caregiver-benefit vendors, Area Agencies on Aging, long-term care insurers, and senior living operators. These have incompatible evidence bars, procurement cycles, integration requirements, economic buyers, and price points. An aims page for a commercialization-readiness award should name one or two.
- **No market segment definition, market count, urgency quantification, pricing, or revenue model appears anywhere.** The CRP significance criterion explicitly folds in commercial potential, value proposition, market pull, customer understanding, and probability of commercialization; roughly two of these are addressed on the page.
- **The competitive environment is characterized generically and no competitor is named or dismissed.** No incumbent is distinguished, and — most importantly for 2026 — the page offers no answer to the obvious skeptical question of why a general-purpose computer-use agent will not close this gap within the award period.
- **Hurdles to adoption are not addressed.** Institutional procurement of an AI system that submits benefit applications on behalf of members raises compliance, liability, delegation-of-authority, and payer-policy hurdles that go unmentioned.
- The magnitude of the gap is asserted rather than sized. No current establishment rate, unresolved-need prevalence, or cost-of-non-establishment figure is given, which weakens both the significance case and the interpretability of Aim 2.

### 2. Investigator(s) — Score: 3

*Assumption stated explicitly: biosketches and supporting personnel documentation were not supplied and are assumed adequate. No team-composition deficiency is inferred from their absence.*

**Strengths**
- The application demonstrates sustained continuity of NIA SBIR execution across Fast-Track Phase I/II and Phase IIB, culminating in a product described as usable and acceptable to families. Successful navigation of a competing renewal is meaningful evidence of both technical and administrative capability.
- The team has evidently retained the domain-specific operational knowledge required to describe eldercare administrative pathways at the level of specificity shown, which is not common among digital health applicants.

**Weaknesses**
- **The aims page contains no team, governance, or operational information at all** — no PI, no co-investigators, no biostatistician, no clinical lead, no commercialization lead, no consultants. While absence of biosketches is not penalized, the page also does not signal the *existence* of the specialized capabilities the aims require.
- **Scope-to-capacity concern.** The work described spans: a longitudinal data ontology across seven state dimensions with four metadata axes; an agentic execution engine handling documents, communications, portals, persistence, and response interpretation; a controlled mixed-methods human-subjects study; a prospective longitudinal outcomes study with independent outcome verification; and institutional buyer research. This is at least four distinct disciplines. Nothing on the page indicates the operational structure that would carry them concurrently.
- No commercialization track record is asserted on the page, and the CRP investigator criterion asks directly how successful the PI has been in commercializing prior technologies. A single clause would address this.

### 3. Innovation — Score: 3

**Strengths**
- The innovation claimed is not "we use AI." It is a specific and defensible reframing: shifting the product's unit of accountability from recommendation to verified establishment. That reframing carries through to the endpoint definition, which is the correct test of whether an innovation claim is genuine.
- The Care Establishment Model — normalizing fragmented inputs into structured longitudinal state across needs, means, funding, services, execution, capacity, and outcomes, with geography, time, source, and provenance — is a substantive architectural proposal, not a wrapper. Provenance and time-stamping in particular reflect real understanding that eldercare operational data decays.
- Bounded execution with permission-gating and verified authority is a meaningfully different design point from both chatbot navigation and fully autonomous agents, and is well-matched to a population where erroneous action carries legal and financial consequences.
- The field-learning byproduct — a grounded operational dataset of where and why care-establishment pathways succeed, stall, or fail — is plausibly the most durable asset the project could create, since that data exists nowhere and cannot be scraped.

**Weaknesses**
- **The page never argues defensibility.** No IP position, no data-rights claim, no barrier to replication, and no statement of why the field-learning dataset compounds. The most valuable innovation in the proposal is mentioned only as an incidental output of Aim 2.
- **No differentiation from general-purpose agentic AI.** The single most likely source of reviewer skepticism — that frontier computer-use agents will perform document handling, portal navigation, and persistent follow-up generically — receives no response. The applicant's real answer (jurisdiction-specific operational state plus outcome-verified field learning) is available but unstated.
- "Closed-loop" is used to describe the platform while consequential actions are described as permission-gated. These are reconcilable — the loop closes on outcome verification, not on autonomy — but the terminology invites a reviewer to overestimate the autonomy claimed and then discount it.
- The innovation is incremental relative to the applicant's own prior awards in one respect: the page does not state which capabilities already exist versus which are new, so the reviewer cannot size the delta being funded.

### 4. Approach — Score: 5

**Strengths**
- The aim sequence is logically sound and correctly ordered: build and verify in the lab, then test in the field, then translate measured performance into commercial terms. Aim 1 earns Aim 2 and Aim 2 earns Aim 3 in principle.
- Explicit go/no-go gating appears at both transitions, and the gate at Aim 1 is a *class-level* gate — only workflow classes meeting technical, safety, and user-acceptance criteria advance. This is the right structural instinct.
- The Aim 1 technical verification dimensions are well chosen and specific: state accuracy, workflow selection, payload completeness, action accuracy, permission compliance, escalation, and material error. This list reflects genuine understanding of where agentic systems fail.
- The primary endpoint is defined with rare discipline. Explicitly enumerating what does *not* count as success — recommendation, referral, submitted application, approval, provider match, scheduled service — forecloses the endpoint-softening that plagues this category.
- Aim 2's secondary measures are well selected for a commercialization award: time to establishment, unresolved needs, failure and delay mechanisms, human escalation, workflow fidelity, safety, and **cost to serve**. Measuring cost to serve prospectively is exactly right and is one of the strongest commercial instincts on the page.
- Interposing a user trust-and-comprehension study between engineering and field deployment is appropriate risk management for a system that acts on families' behalf.

**Weaknesses**
- **No quantitative parameter appears anywhere in the aims.** There is no N for the Aim 1 mixed-methods study, no N of family care episodes for Aim 2, no N of institutional decision-makers for Aim 3, no number of workflow classes, no follow-up duration, no accrual period, no timeline, and no threshold behind any of the stated go/no-go criteria. The aims page is the reviewer's index to the application; without these, the reviewer cannot assess power, feasibility, or ambition, and must defer entirely to a document they read second.
- **"Prespecified" substitutes for specification.** The term is used for workflows, follow-up period, and both go/no-go gates. The reviewer inference is either that criteria do not yet exist, or that the applicant intends to set thresholds after observing data. Either reading materially weakens the risk-retirement argument, because an unstated gate retires nothing.
- **Aim 2 has no comparator, benchmark, or historical baseline.** A single-arm prospective design will produce a verified-establishment rate that cannot be interpreted, cannot be defended to an institutional buyer, and cannot support a value claim. No usual-care arm, navigation-only arm, matched historical cohort, or published benchmark is proposed. This is the most serious methodological weakness on the page and is intrinsic to the design as stated.
- **Outcome ascertainment is undefined.** "Verified establishment" is the primary endpoint, but the page never says by what mechanism verification occurs — family self-report, provider confirmation, award or approval documentation, agent-observed portal state, or independent adjudication. These differ enormously in bias. That "outcome-ascertainment criteria" is listed as a *gate* rather than a *method* compounds the concern.
- **Workflow-class gating and Aim 2 eligibility are not reconciled, and this threatens endpoint validity.** If only a subset of workflow classes clears Aim 1, then episodes whose primary need falls outside that subset either are excluded — biasing enrollment toward tractable cases and inflating the establishment rate — or are enrolled and counted as failures, deflating it. The page does not state which. As written, the headline result is manipulable by an eligibility decision made after Aim 1 concludes.
- **Recruitment is a single clause.** "Prospectively enroll eligible family care episodes through Olera's existing national discovery infrastructure and targeted digital acquisition" carries the entire outcome study. No funnel, conversion assumption, consent rate, accrual rate, retention plan, or attrition mitigation appears. Longitudinal follow-up of families in active care crises is a high-attrition setting, and differential attrition correlated with establishment failure would bias the primary endpoint upward.
- **Geographic and program scope is never bounded.** Administrative execution is inherently jurisdiction-specific — Medicaid waivers, AAA processes, state programs, and provider intake all vary by state. Aim 1 proposes to build and verify workflow classes; Aim 2 proposes national recruitment. The page never reconciles national reach with per-jurisdiction workflow construction, and never says how many programs, states, or provider types are in scope. This is both a feasibility issue and a data-density issue: a nationally dispersed sample at unstated N may yield too few episodes per program-jurisdiction cell to support the field-learning claim.
- **Provider-side participation is unaddressed.** For most care needs, establishment requires a counterparty — a home care agency, adult day program, or facility — to accept the referral and start service. Aim 2's primary endpoint therefore depends on provider responsiveness that Olera does not control, and no provider activation plan, capacity assumption, or attribution logic for provider-caused failure appears.
- **No statistical approach, alternative strategies, or contingencies are stated.** What happens if no workflow class clears the Aim 1 gate? If accrual is 40% of target? If the establishment rate is 20%? None of these branches is described.
- **Aim 1 packs three separable projects** — ontology construction, agent engineering, and a controlled human-subjects study — into a single aim with no internal sequencing or resourcing signal. Reviewers alert to overambition will start here.
- Cost to serve is measured in Aim 2 but never joined to price in Aim 3. Without that linkage the project measures a cost and separately elicits a willingness-to-pay signal but never produces a unit-economics conclusion — the single most decisive analysis for the commercial thesis.

## CRP PROGRESS REPORT

**Strengths**
- Continuity across Fast-Track Phase I/II and Phase IIB is asserted and is credible on its face.
- The predicate capability set is described concretely — modeling household needs and means, identifying benefits and services, matching families with providers, and delivering a family-facing experience — so the reviewer can locate the boundary of what is already built.
- The applicant correctly identifies the residual risk as execution and verification rather than discovery, which is an accurate self-assessment.

**Weaknesses**

For an award whose statutory purpose is enabling third-party investment, this is a significant omission from the applicant's index document.
- **"Validated" is doing unsupported work.** The Background states the product was "shown to be usable and acceptable"; the CRP Objective then calls it "a validated care-navigation technology." Usability and acceptability validation is not validation of navigation accuracy, benefit yield, or outcome. A skeptical reviewer will notice the escalation between paragraphs.
- No statement of which risks the prior awards retired versus which remain, so the reviewer cannot verify that the proposed CRP addresses the right residual risk rather than an arbitrary one.
- No indication of whether any commercialization activity occurred *outside* the scope of prior awards, which the CRP progress report explicitly requests.

## COMMERCIALIZATION PLAN (as inferable from the Specific Aims)

**Strengths**
- The Valley-of-Death logic is coherent and honestly stated: core technology and product-performance risk are currently unresolved, private capital will not finance their resolution, and CRP evidence would move the project to a point where independent capital can fund institution-specific economic validation. This is a legitimate and well-formed Statement of Need argument.
- The applicant explicitly declines to promise institutional ROI within the CRP. Reviewers weary of unfalsifiable ROI claims will credit the restraint.
- Measuring cost to serve inside the research aim rather than modeling it in a spreadsheet is the correct commercial instinct and is uncommon at this stage.
- Aim 3 correctly identifies the right categories of buyer requirement: evidence, integration, procurement, pricing, contracting, and implementation.

**Weaknesses**
- **The buyer is undefined, and defining the buyer is deferred to Aim 3.** Customer discovery inside a commercialization-readiness award inverts the expected maturity ordering.
- **No pricing, revenue model, market count, unit economics, or five-year milestones appear.** The reviewer has no basis to assess whether the market is large enough or the economics workable.
- **Purchase-intent interviews are the weakest admissible form of commercial evidence.** Aim 3 proposes structured validation with decision-makers but no LOI, no design partner agreement, no paid pilot, and no contracted commitment. Stated intent from an interview will not move an institutional investor.
- **The terminal deliverable is a specification, not evidence.** The award concludes with "the post-CRP proof-of-concept needed to measure institution-level economic value" — that is, it defines the study that must later be funded to answer the economic question. A skeptical panelist will ask whether NIH is being asked to fund the evidence required to scope the evidence.
- No IP or defensibility position is stated.
- No competitive or private-sector landscape assessment appears, so the criterion asking whether federal support duplicates well-funded private development cannot be evaluated favorably.

## FUNDRAISING PLAN (as inferable from the Specific Aims)

**Strengths**
- The financing-inflection thesis is clearly articulated and correctly located: the argument is that CRP evidence converts an unresolved-technology-risk story into a scale-and-economics story, which is genuinely the transition private investors underwrite.
- The applicant is explicit that non-SBIR capital will fund full commercialization, consistent with program intent.

**Weaknesses**
- No target raise size, investor type, prior capital raised, or existing investor relationship appears.
- No aim tests, or even instruments, the investability hypothesis. The proposal asserts that evidence will unlock capital but proposes no investor-facing milestone, no named metric an investor would underwrite, and no validation of that assumption with any capital source.
- The inflection argument is weakened by its own honesty: if institution-level economic value remains unmeasured at CRP completion, the investor is being asked to fund the economic study, which is closer to the risk profile the applicant says private capital currently declines.

**Strengths**
- The two go/no-go gates constitute a real management architecture, and gating at the level of workflow class rather than the whole system is a sophisticated choice.

## HUMAN SUBJECTS DESIGN VISIBLE IN THE SPECIFIC AIMS

*Full Human Subjects materials were not supplied and were not reviewed. Consent, privacy, confidentiality, inclusion, IRB arrangements, and monitoring procedures are assumed adequately addressed there and are not scored here. Only issues intrinsic to the research design itself are noted.*

**Strengths**
- Protection is built into the intervention architecture rather than bolted on: consequential actions are permission-gated, required authority is verified before action, and ambiguity is escalated to humans.
- Trust and comprehension are tested in a controlled setting **before** real-world deployment, and only workflow classes meeting safety and user-acceptance criteria advance. This is the correct risk ordering.
- Material error is an explicit measured dimension in Aim 1, and safety is a measured dimension and a gate criterion in Aim 2.

**Weaknesses**
- **No adverse-event taxonomy, monitoring mechanism, or safety stopping rule is visible.** An agent submitting benefit applications can cause consequential harm — a denial that creates a waiting-list penalty, an incorrect attestation on a government form with legal exposure for the applicant, or a delay during which a care need goes unmet. These are intrinsic to the methodology, not procedural, and belong in the research design.
- **Decisional authority is unresolved for the population most likely to be enrolled.** The user is the family caregiver; the beneficiary is the older adult, who may have diminished capacity. Who grants permission for a consequential action, under what legal authority (power of attorney, authorized representative designation), and what happens when caregiver and beneficiary interests diverge, is not addressed. "Required authority is verified" is the right instinct but is asserted without a mechanism.
- The safety-relevant tension between a "closed-loop" system and permission-gating is unresolved in the text, which matters here because the degree of autonomy determines the risk profile.

**Determination for this mock review: CONCERN** (not Major Concern). The design instincts are sound and the gating structure is protective; the gaps are specifiable and are the kind a revision can close.

The assumptions that matter most to an investor (C, D, E, F) are the least retired.

## FEASIBILITY STRESS TEST

**Family acquisition — Weakly supported.** One clause carries the entire recruitment argument. No target N, funnel, conversion assumption, consent rate, accrual rate, or retention plan. Longitudinal follow-up of families in active care crises is a high-attrition setting and attrition is likely correlated with the outcome, which biases the endpoint upward. The underlying asset may well be sufficient; the page does not let a reviewer conclude that.

**Provider acquisition — Weakly supported / not addressed.** The primary endpoint requires a provider or program counterparty to accept and initiate service, yet no provider activation, capacity, or responsiveness plan appears. Establishment failures caused by provider non-response will be attributed to the intervention with no way to separate them.

**Workforce acquisition — Not applicable as written.** This CareNavigator-only aims page makes no workforce claim. Noted only so the applicant can confirm the aims page is scoped consistently with the rest of the package.

**Market activation — Cannot be assessed; the absence is the finding.** No geography, state count, program count, or market is named anywhere. Since administrative execution is jurisdiction-specific, an unbounded scope is the single largest unpriced feasibility risk in Aim 1.

**Technical workload — Plausible but vulnerable.** The Care Establishment Model as described (seven state dimensions plus geography, time, source, and provenance) is a substantial data-infrastructure undertaking; the agentic execution layer is a second one; the controlled user study is a third. Concurrent delivery within a CRP period is possible only with a scope boundary that is not stated.

**Human-subjects workload — Plausible but vulnerable.** Assuming the Human Subjects materials are adequate, the operational burden remains heavy: prospective consent, longitudinal follow-up, independent outcome verification per episode, and mixed-methods qualitative work with actual users, all concurrent with active engineering.

**Commercial workload — Credible.** Aim 3 as scoped — structured interviews, an evidence package, and a specification — is comfortably achievable. This is worth noting precisely because Aim 3 is the *most* feasible and the *least* commercially decisive aim in the application. The workload is available to do more.

## LIKELY DISCUSSION DYNAMICS

The assigned primary reviewer will open favorably. The care-establishment framing reads as unusually clear-sighted, and the endpoint definition will be quoted approvingly — panels reward applicants who make their own success harder to claim. The primary will then pivot to the absence of quantification and will likely say some version of *"I could not tell how large any of this is."*

The secondary reviewer will carry three memorable objections: no N anywhere, no comparator in Aim 2, and a buyer described only as "organizations responsible for populations with unresolved eldercare needs." The third of these will draw the sharpest reaction, because CRP reviewers are primed by the funding opportunity to ask whether the award reaches contracting.

At discussion, the debate will concentrate on Aim 3 and will split along a genuine philosophical line. One camp will argue that the applicant is being appropriately rigorous — refusing to promise ROI they cannot measure, and correctly identifying that buyer-tested requirements must precede an economic study. The other will argue that a CRP exists specifically to close the last commercial gap, and that an award concluding with a specification of the study that must next be funded has moved the applicant one step, not across the valley. The skeptical, time-constrained reviewer's belief weakens at two identifiable sentences. This reviewer will also ask, unprompted, why a frontier computer-use agent will not do this generically within the project period, and will find no answer on the page.

Convergence is likely in the 3–5 band, settling at 4. Significance and Innovation will draw the widest spread (2–4); Approach will be the most uniformly criticized criterion, with 4–6 all defensible depending on how much credit each reviewer extends to an unseen Research Strategy.

## OVERALL IMPACT SCORE: 4 (Very Good)

**Why this score.** The application targets the correct residual problem in its category, defines success more strictly than its competitors do, sequences its risk retirement properly, and builds protection into the intervention rather than around it. It is backed by demonstrated NIA SBIR execution through a competing renewal. Against that, the page makes no quantitative commitment of any kind, proposes an uninterpretable single-arm primary comparison, defers customer identification into the final aim, and concludes the award with a specification of the evidence that a later, privately funded study must produce. The project is very likely to yield honest and useful findings; it is not yet shown to yield the specific evidence a purchaser or an investor would act on.

**Why not one point better (3).** A 3 would require the reviewer to believe that the award produces evidence that unlocks capital. Three things prevent that belief on this page: the buyer is unnamed, so no one can say which evidence matters; Aim 2 has no reference point, so the headline result cannot be characterized as good; and the commercial deliverable is a specification rather than a validated economic or contractual outcome. Additionally, no risk-retirement claim can be checked, because every go/no-go gate withholds its threshold. A reviewer cannot award "Excellent" to a risk-retirement plan whose criteria are not disclosed.

**Why not one point worse (5).** A 5 would imply the underlying plan is merely adequate. It is better than that. The care-establishment thesis is correct and non-obvious; the endpoint definition is more rigorous than most funded work in this space and would produce a genuinely credible result if the design questions were closed; the gating architecture is well-placed; safety is engineered rather than promised; cost to serve is measured rather than modeled; and the applicant has already delivered through Phase IIB on the predicate technology. Most of the deficits are omissions on a two-page document rather than defects in the underlying program, and the two that are genuinely structural — the missing comparator and the deferred buyer — are both correctable without changing the aims' architecture.

**Practical note:** the distance between this score and a 3 is almost entirely recoverable within this single page. That is unusual and worth acting on.

# FINAL APPLICANT DIAGNOSTIC

*Provided only after scores were locked.*

## Must fix in the Specific Aims page before submission

### 1. Name the buyer, and move buyer identification out of Aim 3

- **Section:** CRP Objective and Aim 3
- **Problem:** The buyer is "organizations responsible for populations with unresolved eldercare needs" — a phrase spanning MA plans, Medicaid MCOs, ACOs, health systems, employer caregiver-benefit vendors, AAAs, and LTC insurers, whose evidence bars and procurement paths are mutually incompatible. Aim 3 proposes to determine who they are and what they pay.
- **Reviewer inference:** *Customer discovery inside a CRP. They do not know who buys this yet.*
- **Scoring consequence:** This is the largest single driver of the Significance score and a major contributor to Overall Impact. It is the objection most likely to dominate discussion.
- **Minimum change:** Name one primary and at most one secondary buyer segment in the CRP Objective. Reframe Aim 3 from *determining* requirements to *validating and contracting against* the requirements of a named segment. If a specific institution or category is already in conversation, say so in six words.

### 2. Put numbers on the page

- **Section:** All three aims
- **Problem:** Not a single N, threshold, duration, rate, or date appears in two pages.
- **Reviewer inference:** *They have not sized this, or they do not want me to check the arithmetic.*
- **Scoring consequence:** Primary driver of the Approach score of 5; also suppresses Investigator and Environment, because the reviewer cannot judge whether the team and resources match the work.
- **Minimum change:** Add, at minimum: number of workflow classes targeted in Aim 1; N for the Aim 1 user study; N of family care episodes for Aim 2; the follow-up window in months; N of institutional decision-makers in Aim 3; and the total project duration. Six numbers. They will fit in the space freed by item 10.

### 3. Give Aim 2 a reference point

- **Section:** Aim 2, primary endpoint
- **Problem:** A single-arm design with no comparator, benchmark, or historical baseline.
- **Reviewer inference:** *Whatever rate they report, I will not know whether it is good.*
- **Scoring consequence:** Structural Approach weakness that no Research Strategy language can repair, because the design is stated here. Also undermines the commercial thesis, since a rate without a reference cannot be sold.
- **Minimum change:** One clause. Either (a) a concurrent navigation-only or usual-care comparison arm, (b) a matched historical baseline from Olera's own prior data, or (c) an explicitly stated benchmark establishment rate drawn from literature, against which the endpoint will be tested. Option (b) is likely cheapest and is defensible if the historical cohort is described.

### 4. Replace "prespecified" with at least one actual specification per gate

- **Section:** Aim 1 advancement criteria; Aim 2 advancement criteria
- **Problem:** Both go/no-go gates withhold their thresholds; "prespecified" is used four to five times as a placeholder.
- **Reviewer inference:** *The criteria may not exist yet, or will be set after the data are in.*
- **Scoring consequence:** Neutralizes the risk-retirement argument, which is the entire premise of a CRP. Contributes directly to Approach and to Overall Impact.
- **Minimum change:** State one hard threshold per gate. For example: workflow classes advance at ≥X% action accuracy with zero permission violations and ≤Y% material error; Aim 3 proceeds at ≥Z verified establishments with no unresolved safety signal. Exact values matter less than demonstrating that thresholds exist and were set in advance.

### 5. Name the outcome-ascertainment method

- **Section:** Aim 2, primary endpoint
- **Problem:** "Verified establishment" never says verified how.
- **Reviewer inference:** *This is probably family self-report, which they know is weak, which is why it is not stated.*
- **Scoring consequence:** Directly threatens the credibility of the strongest feature of the application. An excellent endpoint definition with an unstated measurement method invites the reviewer to assume the weakest one.
- **Minimum change:** One clause naming the hierarchy — e.g., documentary confirmation (award letter, service start record, provider confirmation) as primary, with structured family confirmation as fallback, and how discordance is adjudicated.

### 6. Bound the scope of Aim 1

- **Section:** Aim 1, Care Establishment Model and workflow classes
- **Problem:** No geography, state count, program count, or provider type is named, while the described work is inherently jurisdiction-specific. Aim 2 then asserts national recruitment.
- **Reviewer inference:** *They intend to build workflows for the entire country. That is not fundable in this period.*
- **Scoring consequence:** Feasibility and overambition — a top Approach objection and a likely trigger for a "too ambitious for the scope" comment at discussion.
- **Minimum change:** Name the program families and the number of jurisdictions in scope for Aim 1, and state whether Aim 2 recruitment is national or restricted to those jurisdictions. If national recruitment with restricted workflow coverage is intended, say so and explain how eligibility is handled — which also resolves item 7.

### 7. Reconcile workflow-class gating with Aim 2 eligibility

- **Section:** Aim 1 → Aim 2 transition
- **Problem:** If only some workflow classes clear the gate, it is unstated whether episodes outside those classes are excluded (inflating the establishment rate through selection) or enrolled and counted as failures (deflating it).
- **Reviewer inference:** *The headline number can be moved by an eligibility decision made after they see the Aim 1 results.*
- **Scoring consequence:** A rigor and bias objection landing on the application's flagship endpoint. Sophisticated reviewers will find this quickly.
- **Minimum change:** One sentence stating the eligibility rule and committing to it in advance, plus a commitment to report the establishment rate both within covered classes and across all enrolled episodes.

### 8. Join measured cost to serve to buyer-indicated price

- **Section:** Aim 2 (cost to serve) and Aim 3 (pricing requirements)
- **Problem:** Both halves of the unit-economics equation are collected; they are never multiplied.
- **Reviewer inference:** *They will finish the award still unable to state a gross margin.*
- **Scoring consequence:** The most efficient available improvement to the Commercialization assessment — it converts two disconnected measurements into the one analysis an investor needs.
- **Minimum change:** Add to Aim 3 an explicit deliverable: a unit-economics statement comparing measured cost to serve against validated price points at the named buyer segment, with the scale assumptions that would make it viable.

### 9. Strengthen the Aim 3 commercial endpoint beyond interviews

- **Section:** Aim 3
- **Problem:** The terminal commercial deliverable is a specification plus interview-derived requirements. Stated purchase intent is the weakest admissible commercial evidence.
- **Reviewer inference:** *This is a research plan for a commercialization award. The financing inflection is asserted, not produced.*
- **Scoring consequence:** The central driver of the Overall Impact ceiling and the likely focus of panel disagreement.
- **Minimum change:** Commit to at least one binding artifact as an Aim 3 milestone — a signed design-partner agreement, a letter of intent contingent on stated performance, or a paid pilot commitment. One such artifact does more for the CRP argument than the entire evidence package, because it converts intent into behavior. If a paid pilot within the period is realistic, make it the aim's headline.

### 10. Add a traction sentence and a defensibility clause; cut the Vision paragraph in half

- **Section:** Background / CRP Objective / Vision
- **Problem:** The page carries no commercialization traction of any kind, no IP or defensibility claim, and no response to general-purpose agentic AI — while spending roughly 120 words on a Vision paragraph that restates the CRP Objective.
- **Reviewer inference:** *No revenue, no customers, no IP, and nothing stopping a frontier lab from doing this. Also, they had space and used it to repeat themselves.*
- **Scoring consequence:** Suppresses Innovation (defensibility) and the Progress Report and Environment assessments, all cheaply.
- **Minimum change:** Cut the Vision paragraph to two sentences. Use the recovered space for (a) one sentence of traction — users served, institutional conversations, IP filed, or capital raised; (b) one clause replacing "validated" with what was actually validated and in whom; and (c) one sentence stating why outcome-verified, jurisdiction-specific execution data is not replicable by general-purpose agents.

## Do not waste space fixing

- **Naming consultants, advisors, or personnel.** Biosketches carry this; the aims page gains nothing.
- **Market-size statistics.** TAM figures and unclaimed-benefit totals belong in the Commercialization Plan. They will not improve the Significance score here and they consume the lines the sample sizes need.
- **Technical detail on models, architectures, or AI methods.** The reviewers who matter will not be persuaded by implementation detail, and the historical record for this panel shows AI method description attracting criticism rather than credit.
- **Enumerating the full Care Establishment Model ontology.** The seven state dimensions and four metadata axes can be compressed to a single characterization. Reviewers need the scope boundary, not the schema.
- **A Human Subjects protocol summary.** Consent, privacy, IRB, and inclusion belong in the Human Subjects materials. The only human-subjects content that earns its place here is the adverse-event and stopping-rule architecture (which is a design element) and the decisional-authority mechanism for beneficiaries with diminished capacity.
- **Literature citations or background epidemiology.** The problem framing is already the strongest prose on the page and does not need external support to be believed.
- **Restating the CRP program's own purpose.** The financing-inflection argument is made once effectively; the Vision paragraph makes it a second time at length.
