# Provider copy, under the microscope

Everything a provider hears or reads, in one place, extracted from the ladder
rather than gathered by memory — so a rung nobody thought of is still here.

**Scope.** The providers ladder only. Students, advisors, orgs, events and
professors are a separate sitting.

Round 1: the inventory and what is wrong with it. Nothing has been rewritten
yet. Read the findings, then the copy, and mark up what you want changed.

---

## Findings, worst first

### F1 · A provider who never replies gets the same email seven times 🔴

Rounds 1 through 7 of the cold block are one email, sent again. Same subject
line, same body, same opening. Same for the call: the same voicemail script,
seven times, over a fortnight.

The subject line is the worst of it. **Following up — Student Caregiver
Program at {university}** arrives seven times in one thread, so by round three
the thread reads as a machine, which is the one thing a personal email from a
named office must not do.

This is also true of the onboarding block, where it matters more: these are
providers who have already said yes.

**What a fix looks like.** Not seven new emails. Three or four distinct
moves, reused: the resend, the short bump, the different angle, and the
last one. The last one in particular should say it is the last one — a
"I will stop here unless I hear from you" earns more replies than the six
before it combined, and it is the honest thing to send.

### F2 · The flyer names a price. Three other numbers are on file 🔴

The flyer linked in the first email and every follow-up says:

> Free to browse and interview. **$200 once per hire** — refunded if they work
> under 15 hours.

`C1` in the open-conflicts register lists the price as undecided with four
readings: **$250+ per hire** (your meeting), **$100 from both parties**
(shipped code), **$0** (your "I took out all paywalls, we are pre-pilot"), and
a billing trigger of 6+ shifts from the master funnel. The pilot terms draft
proposes **$250**.

`D-011` deliberately kept the price out of the pack email, and there is a
committed check that fails if a price appears in it. Meanwhile the flyer has
been answering the question at $200 for every provider who clicked the link.

**This is not a copy problem and cannot be fixed by rewriting.** The number has
to be decided. Until it is, every provider we contact is being quoted a price
nobody has agreed.

### F3 · The flyer and the emails sell different things 🟠

The flyer leads with the provider's problem:

> Vetted pre-health student caregivers **for the recurring shifts you can't
> fill.**

The emails lead with the student's need:

> We work with pre-health students … who **want paid, hands-on caregiving
> experience** before they apply to professional school.

Both are true. The flyer's framing is the stronger one — it opens on
something the agency already loses sleep over, where the email opens on
something that is our problem, not theirs. The email gets to coverage
eventually, in the third sentence, hedged as "they are looking for shifts that
fit around their classes".

Worth deciding which we lead with, and then leading with it everywhere.

### F4 · The programme email says the same thing twice 🟠

Follow-up email, two sentences apart:

> … and they come to you **screened and ready**.
>
> **They come to you screened.** You interview and hire the ones you want …

The second paragraph is lifted verbatim from the first email, where it reads
well. Pasted under a sentence that already said it, it reads as carelessness.

### F5 · "Would you like to hear more?" is a weak ask 🟠

It is the close of both the first email and every follow-up. It asks for
permission to send more email. A provider who is mildly interested has to
imagine what comes next and decide they want it.

Compare the onboarding follow-up, which is much better:

> Just one question: are you ready for us to send your first student? If yes,
> a one-line reply is all I need.

That is a real question with a one-word answer. The cold email deserves the
same treatment.

### F6 · Every call script is a cold open with no second move 🟡

Each script is one paragraph: what to say if they pick up and are the right
person. None covers the three things that actually happen — a gatekeeper, a
voicemail, or "send me an email". The board now has voicemail and no-answer
buttons on every calling rung, so the screen knows those outcomes exist while
the script pretends they do not.

A voicemail script in particular is worth writing once: it is left more often
than any other thing we say.

### F7 · Two names for the same programme 🟡

"Student Caregiver Program" in the emails. "Olera's Student Caregiver
Program" on the flyer title. "his Student Caregiver Program" on the step 1
call. Small, but it is the name of the thing, and it should be one name.

### F8 · The pack is long, and the ask is buried at the bottom 🟡

The pack email runs four sections. The one thing we want back — *reply and
tell me you are ready* — is the fourth heading, after the terms. Then a
further paragraph about getting on a call arrives underneath it, which softens
the ask it follows.

It may be right to keep the pack long, because it is the document that stops
anybody having to explain the programme again. But the ask should be at the
top as well as the bottom.

## The copy

## 1. Call to confirm the right contact
*Step 1*

**What it is:** A short call to confirm who we should be talking to, and how to reach them.
**Why:** The research gives us a name and an address. Only the call proves they are the right ones.

**Steps shown to the operator:**
1. Call the main line.
2. Ask who handles this and for their email.
3. Type it in and log the call.

### Call script

```
"Hi, this is [your name] from Dr. DuBose's office, calling about his Student Caregiver Program. I'd like to send your team the details — what's the best address?"
```

## 2. Send the program info

**Why:** It comes from a real person, so replies land in your inbox.

**Steps shown to the operator:**
1. Copy the email below.
2. Check the flyer link opens.
3. Send it, then log it.

## 4. Send the onboarding pack
*Step 4*

**What it is:** How the programme works, what they want in a caregiver, the pilot terms, and one question back.
**Why:** They have said yes. This is the email that means nobody has to explain it again.

**Steps shown to the operator:**
1. Create their portal account and paste the link in below.
2. Copy the email and read it through before you send it.
3. Attach the pilot terms — they are for review, not for signing.
4. Send it, then log it. Chasing the reply is the next rung.

### Email

**Subject:** Everything you need — Student Caregiver Program at {university}

```
Hi {first},

Good to hear from you. Here is the whole thing.

HOW IT WORKS
When a pre-health student near {org} is ready, we send them over — by email and by text, and they are waiting in your portal too. Some will ring your office directly and say they came through the Student Caregiver Program.

Each student arrives as one page: what they are studying, when they can work, what they have done before, and a short video. You invite the ones you want to interview, and you hire on your own terms.

  1. We tell you a student is ready
  2. You invite them to interview
  3. You hire the ones you want
  4. We confirm the hire with you and with them

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING FOR
Tell us and we will only send students who fit — hours, shift types, certifications, anything you will not move on. The easiest thing is to reply to this email and say it in your own words; we will set it up on our side. If you would rather do it yourself, it is all in your portal: {portal_link}

THE TERMS, FOR YOUR REVIEW
Attached. Nothing to sign, and no obligation to carry on — this is a pilot. We will keep sending you students until you hire one and the placement works out. If you like working with our students after that, we agree formal terms then rather than now.

ONE THING BACK FROM YOU
Reply and tell me you are ready to receive your first student and you are clear on what happens when one arrives. If anything is unclear, reply with the question instead and I will answer it here.

When your first student is ready I will get on a call with you then and we will go through reviewing them and inviting them to interview together. And if you would rather talk any of it through sooner — now, at the first student, or later — just say the word.

Best,
[your name]
Dr. Logan DuBose's office · Olera
```

**Attachment:** Pilot terms, for their review (`pilot-terms`)

## 6. The provider flyer

Linked as `{flyer}` from the programme email and every cold follow-up.

**Headline**

> Vetted pre-health student caregivers for the recurring shifts you can't fill.

**Subhead**

> Olera recruits and vets local pre-nursing and pre-medical students and
> matches them to your agency as caregivers. They want supervised hours,
> mentorship, and recommendation letters for health school, so they commit to
> a semester of recurring availability and take the work seriously. You get

**How it works**

1. Create your free account and review candidates.

3. We match a vetted student whose availability fits.

**What vetted means**

- Screened pre-nursing and pre-medical students.
- Committed to a semester of recurring availability.

## Not provider-facing

### Sweep Google Maps for missing agencies
*Step 9 · branch "mapsweep"*

**What it is:** Search the map pack around campus and add the home care agencies the directory never had.
**Why:** The directory can only give us agencies it has heard of. The map pack has ones it has not, and those stay invisible until somebody looks.

**Steps shown to the operator:**
1. Open Google Maps at the campus, below.
2. Search each of: home care, home health, senior care, caregiver agency.
3. For each result near campus, check it against the provider list. Match on phone and street address, not name — franchises repeat names.
4. Add the ones that pass the test below with Add a provider, then come back here.

### Disclosure: what counts as one worth adding

```
Add an agency when all four are true:

  1. It sends caregivers to someone's home. Not a facility, not hospital staffing, not medical supply. Home care agencies only.
  2. Its address is near campus, inside the area we recruit from.
  3. It has a phone number that works, or a website.
  4. No provider already on the board shares its phone number or its street address.

If you are unsure on any of the four, leave it out and say so in the note. A provider added wrongly costs somebody a research rung and a call.
```
