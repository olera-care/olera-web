# Provider copy — second draft

Everything rewritten against the principles: clear, direct, plain, mature,
concise. Personal rather than marketed. One ask per message, and the ask is
always something a busy person can do in ten seconds.

Nothing here is shipped. Read it, mark it up, and I will put the agreed
version into the ladder.

---

## The rules I wrote to

1. **One ask per message, and it is always a reply.** Never two asks. Never
   an ask that requires a decision they have not got enough information to
   make.
2. **The first sentence says who I am and why I am writing.** No warm-up.
3. **Short.** The cold emails are under 120 words. Five of the seven
   follow-ups are under 50.
4. **No marketing register.** No *excited to*, no *reach out*, no *seamless*,
   no *partner with you on this journey*, no exclamation marks, no three-item
   rhetorical lists. If a sentence would not survive being read aloud to a
   busy office manager, it is out.
5. **It reads as one person writing to another.** A pre-health student or
   Logan, from an office, not a company with a content calendar.
6. **The provider is the employer and the copy never blurs that.** They
   interview, they decide, they onboard. We recruit and screen.
7. **An easy no is offered wherever it is credible.** *If it is not a fit,
   say so and I will close it off* earns more replies than another paragraph
   of benefits, and it keeps the list clean.

### On the brackets

`{first}`, `{org}`, `{university}`, `{portal_link}` and `{flyer}` are **filled
in by the board before the admin ever sees them** — the screen shows *Hi
Dana,*, not *Hi {first},*. They only look like tokens in this document
because it prints the template.

So I have kept those and used square brackets only for what the app cannot
know: **[your name]** and **[Calendly link]**. Those are the two the admin has
to type, and brackets make that obvious at a glance. If you would rather every
variable were a bracket the admin fills by hand, say so — it is a small change,
but it would mean retyping the agency name and the university on every email.

## The asks, rung by rung

Each message exists to move the provider one rung. Here is the whole ladder as
a single ask per step.

| Rung | The one ask |
|---|---|
| Confirming call | *Who should I send the details to, and what is the best email?* |
| Programme email | *If you would like to try it with one student, reply and I will set it up.* |
| Cold follow-up 1–7 | Same ask, said seven different ways, ending with an easy no |
| Onboarding pack | *Reply and tell me you are ready for your first student.* |
| Onboarding follow-up 1–7 | Same ask, plus an offer to do the setup for them |

## Subject lines

The single biggest change. Seven identical subject lines in one thread is the
clearest signal a human is not writing them.

| # | Subject | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| Programme email | **Student caregivers from {university}** | Concrete, no hype, says what it is in four words. Nothing to decode. |
| Follow-up 1 | *Re: Student caregivers from {university}* | Genuine thread reply. Keeps it in the same conversation. |
| Follow-up 2 | **Recurring shifts — students from {university}** | New angle, so a new line. Leads with their problem. |
| Follow-up 3 | **Am I writing to the right person?** | Answerable in five words. Often forwarded rather than answered, which is the point. |
| Follow-up 4 | **Your first student is free** | The strongest fact we have, said plainly and only once. |
| Follow-up 5 | *Re: Your first student is free* | A one-line bump belongs in the thread it bumps. |
| Follow-up 6 | **Happy to walk you through it** | Offers the lower-effort path for people who do not read email. |
| Follow-up 7 | **Closing this off** | Honest, and the highest-reply email in most sequences. |
| Onboarding pack | **Student Caregiver Program — everything in one place** | Signals it is the reference email, the one to keep. |
| Onboarding 1 | *Re: Student Caregiver Program — everything in one place* | Thread reply. |
| Onboarding 2 | **Ready for your first student?** | The ask, as the subject. |

# The copy

## 1 · Confirming call

**Ask:** the right person and their email.

> Hi, my name is [your name]. I'm calling from Dr. Logan DuBose's office about
> the Student Caregiver Program at {university}.
>
> We work with pre-health students there — pre-med, pre-nursing, pre-PA — who
> are looking for paid caregiving shifts, and we place them with agencies like
> yours. I'd like to send over the details.
>
> Who's the best person for that, and what email should I use?

**If they ask what it is:**

> Students commit to recurring hours for a semester, you interview and hire
> them yourself, and the first one is free. The overview explains it better
> than I can on the phone — can I send it over?

**If you get voicemail:**

> Hi, this is [your name] from Dr. Logan DuBose's office, calling about the
> Student Caregiver Program at {university}. We place screened pre-health
> students into caregiving shifts. I'll send the details by email as well —
> if there's a better address for that, my number is [your number]. Thanks.

## 2 · Programme email

**Attach the one-page overview before you send.** The email points at it, so
it has to be there.

**Subject:** Student caregivers from {university}

> Hi {first},
>
> I work with Dr. Logan DuBose on the Student Caregiver Program at
> {university}. We recruit and screen pre-health students there — pre-med,
> pre-nursing, pre-PA — who want paid caregiving hours before they apply to
> professional school, and we place them with home care agencies nearby.
>
> I've attached a one-page overview. The short version:
>
> - They commit to recurring availability for a semester, so they cover the
>   shifts that are hardest to fill.
> - They reach you screened, on one page: availability, experience, and a
>   short video.
> - You interview and hire on your own terms. You are the employer.
> - Your first student is free, and there is nothing to sign to start.
>
> If you'd like to try it with one student, reply and I'll set it up.
>
> [your name]
> Dr. Logan DuBose's office · Olera

## 6 · The provider flyer — rebuilt

**Built, not proposed.** `lib/program-pdf/Template.tsx` is rewritten and the
copy lives in `lib/program-pdf/configs/`. Two pages now.

**Page one, the offer.** A deep emerald band across the top, then the
headline *Pre-health students, ready for your hardest shifts*, four reasons
agencies participate as cards, three numbered steps, what vetted means, and a
price panel that puts **Free** and **$250 per confirmed hire** side by side
under their own labels. It closes on the ask and a QR.

**Page two, the people.** *Why this program exists* in Logan's words with his
photo, then *what happens after you reply* as three steps, then Chantel,
Graize and Sara with photos, roles, emails and two lines each. The old flyer
put two small headshots in a footer; this makes the team the reason to trust
the offer.

### The price, now stated

The flyer says **your first hire is free, $250 per confirmed hire after
that**, with the reason: the fee pays the staff who run the university
funnels. That is what you wrote, and it settles the flyer's half of `C1`.

**It does not match the emails in this document**, which say *we agree terms
then, based on what the students are worth to you* and name no number. Both
can be true — the flyer carries the number, the email does not — but the
email's *we agree terms then* implies the number is open when the attachment
says it is $250. Worth deciding which reading you want before this goes out.

## What shipping this will touch

The ladder holds four assertions that pin the current wording. They were
written to protect decisions, and three of them still protect the right thing
once the phrasing moves:

| Assertion | What happens |
|---|---|
| *no price in the email* | Still passes. Nothing here quotes a number. |
| *the terms are for review, not signature* | Looks for the exact string `Nothing to sign`. The new pack says it — keep, but make it case-insensitive so a later edit does not break it silently. |
| *it says there is no obligation* | The phrase *no obligation* is gone; the meaning is carried by *nothing is owed before that, and nothing is signed now*. Needs rewriting to test the decision rather than the words. |
| *every cold email asks for a reply instead* | Looks for `hear more` or `send you the details`. Both phrases are gone. Should become: every cold email contains the word *reply*. |

And one guard is worth keeping exactly as it is: **no cold rung asks for
time.** The Calendly link appears twice above, both times as an alternative to
replying, never as the ask.
